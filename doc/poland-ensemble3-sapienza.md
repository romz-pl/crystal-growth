# Sapienza University of Rome and Compound Semiconductor Bulk Crystal Growth: A Technical and Economic Assessment for a Polish Wafer Manufacturing Ecosystem

## Executive Summary

This report evaluates Sapienza University of Rome — Europe's largest university by enrollment and one of Italy's leading research institutions — as a candidate research, development, and industrial partner for a Polish initiative to build an advanced compound semiconductor (III-V, III-N, and wide-bandgap) bulk crystal growth and wafer manufacturing ecosystem. The analysis distinguishes carefully between two things that are frequently conflated in policy discussions: (1) semiconductor *physics and materials science* generally, where Sapienza has genuine strength, and (2) *bulk crystal growth engineering* — the melt- and vapor-based technologies (Czochralski, Bridgman/VGF, PVT/sublimation, HVPE, ammonothermal, Kyropoulos) used to produce boules and ingots that are subsequently sliced into wafers — where Sapienza has essentially no direct institutional footprint.

**Bottom line:** Sapienza is a strong partner for upstream science (epitaxial growth, nanostructures, defect physics, characterization, device physics, computational modelling) and for training high-caliber personnel, but it is **not** a bulk crystal growth institution in the sense that CNR-IMEM (Parma), the Leibniz Institute for Crystal Growth (IKZ, Berlin), or Fraunhofer IISB (Erlangen) are. A Polish ecosystem-building strategy that treats Sapienza as a bulk-growth technology source would be built on a false premise. Sapienza's realistic role is as a *complementary* science and talent partner within a consortium anchored by institutions and companies that actually operate boule-growth furnaces and wafering lines — several of which exist in Poland already (Institute of Electronic Materials Technology / ITME, Institute of High Pressure Physics PAS / UNIPRESS, Ammono, and others).

## 1. Scope and Methodology

"Bulk crystal growth and wafer technologies" is a specific sub-discipline of materials engineering concerned with:

- **Melt growth**: Czochralski (CZ), Liquid-Encapsulated Czochralski (LEC), Vertical/Horizontal Bridgman (VB/HB), Vertical Gradient Freeze (VGF), Kyropoulos, Edge-defined Film-fed Growth (EFG).
- **Vapor growth**: Physical Vapor Transport / sublimation (PVT, dominant for SiC and AlN), Hydride Vapor Phase Epitaxy (HVPE) used both for thick GaN templates and as a quasi-bulk method.
- **Solution/high-pressure growth**: Ammonothermal GaN, high-nitrogen-pressure solution growth (HNPSG, the Unipress/Ammono route), flux methods.
- **Downstream wafering**: ingot slicing (wire sawing), lapping, polishing (CMP), edge-grinding, epi-ready surface preparation, and metrology (XRD rocking curves, etch-pit density, resistivity mapping).

This is distinct from, though feeding into, epitaxial thin-film growth (MOCVD, MBE, HVPE-as-epitaxy) and device fabrication, which are separate (if adjacent) disciplines. The assessment below applies this precise definition when scoring Sapienza's relevant capabilities, since conflating "semiconductor research" broadly with "bulk crystal growth" specifically is the most common — and most consequential — error in this kind of institutional due diligence.

Findings are based on a review of Sapienza's published research output, departmental structure, laboratory descriptions, and comparison against the recognized European centers of bulk crystal growth excellence.

## 2. Sapienza University of Rome: Institutional Profile

Sapienza, founded in 1303, is <cite index="5-1">one of Europe's oldest and largest academic institutions, a premier public research university with over 115,000 students, 3,000 professors, and more than 50 departments</cite>, and it is particularly recognized in <cite index="5-1">engineering, natural and physical sciences, humanities, and the social sciences</cite>. Relevant units include the Department of Physics, the Department of Basic and Applied Sciences for Engineering (SBAI), and various interdisciplinary nanoscience and photonics laboratories.

### 2.1 Documented semiconductor-related strengths

Based on current published output, Sapienza's genuine semiconductor-adjacent competencies cluster in four areas:

**(a) III-V and III-N nanowire epitaxy and quantum photonics.**
Groups such as the Nanospectroscopy Lab (Prof. Marta De Luca, building on earlier work under Prof. A. Polimeni) work on vapor–liquid–solid (VLS) growth of quantum-dot-embedded nanowires (e.g., GaAsP dots in GaP nanowires) and bandgap engineering of GaAsN nanowires on silicon, targeting single-photon emitters for quantum photonic devices. This is high-quality *nanoscale epitaxial* materials science, not bulk crystal growth — nanowire VLS growth uses metal-catalyzed vapor-phase deposition at the nanometer/micrometer scale, an entirely different reactor technology, growth kinetics regime, and defect physics from boule growth.

**(b) 2D materials and van der Waals heterostructure physics.**
Sapienza's Physics Department has produced notable work on energy-transfer mechanisms between graphene and 2D semiconductors relevant to next-generation photodetectors, and on percolation dynamics in crystalline phase-change materials. This is fundamental condensed-matter physics with device relevance, but again operates at the thin-film/2D-flake scale, not bulk ingot scale.

**(c) Phase-change and chalcogenide materials for memory and neuromorphic devices.**
Collaborative work (e.g., with RWTH Aachen, Università di Milano-Bicocca) on wafer-scale-grown textured heterostructure thin films (TiTe₂/Ge₂Sb₂Te₅) for phase-change memory shows Sapienza contributes to *thin-film* wafer-scale deposition and ab-initio modelling of these material systems — relevant to memory/neuromorphic device fabrication, not to growing the underlying semiconductor substrate wafers themselves.

**(d) Infrared/THz spectroscopy and nanoscale characterization instrumentation.**
The IRS (Infrared and Raman Spectroscopy / nanospectroscopy) laboratory collaborates with partners to access <cite index="3-1">infrared synchrotron radiation, single-crystal and thin-film material growth, electromagnetic simulations and nanofabrication technologies</cite> — indicating Sapienza's own growth capability is accessed largely *through partners*, with Sapienza providing advanced optical/vibrational characterization (photothermal IR nanospectroscopy combined with electrostatic AFM, THz techniques) rather than the growth infrastructure itself.

### 2.2 What is notably absent

No evidence was found of:
- An operating Czochralski, Bridgman/VGF, PVT/sublimation, HVPE-for-bulk, or ammonothermal reactor line at Sapienza dedicated to producing compound semiconductor boules or ingots (GaAs, InP, GaSb, SiC, GaN, AlN, Ga₂O₃).
- Wafering/slicing/polishing infrastructure (wire saws, CMP lines) of the kind operated by dedicated crystal-growth institutes.
- Publication output in the core bulk-growth journals (*Journal of Crystal Growth*, *Journal of Crystal Growth & Design*) under Sapienza-affiliated bulk-growth-specific research programs comparable to, e.g., CNR-IMEM's decades of GaAs/InP/SiC boule work.
- A named bulk-crystal-growth research group or PI comparable in profile to figures such as Roberto Fornari (CNR-IMEM → IKZ Berlin → University of Parma), whose career exemplifies the Italian bulk/epitaxial semiconductor lineage but is institutionally centered at **Parma and CNR**, not Sapienza.

This is the central finding of the report and should shape any partnership strategy.

## 3. Where Italy's Actual Bulk Crystal Growth Expertise Resides

For calibration, it is useful to note where in Italy this capability *does* exist, since a Poland–Italy semiconductor partnership need not be limited to Sapienza and may in fact be better served by targeting these institutions directly or in combination with Sapienza:

- **CNR-IMEM (Institute of Materials for Electronics and Magnetism), Parma** — Italy's historical center for bulk and epitaxial III-V/oxide semiconductor growth (GaAs, InP, GaN, AlN, InGaN, Ga₂O₃), the institutional home of Roberto Fornari for over two decades and still active in Ga₂O₃ and related wide-bandgap oxide crystal/epitaxy work.
- **CNR-IMM (Institute for Microelectronics and Microsystems), Catania** — Italy's leading center for SiC materials and power device technology, with strong ties to STMicroelectronics' Catania SiC fab, covering hetero-epitaxial 3C-SiC on Si, SiC MEMS, and wide-bandgap power device processing.
- **University of Parma** (Fornari's current institutional home) — now the academic anchor for Ga₂O₃ bulk/epitaxial growth research in Italy, publishing on Czochralski-related β-Ga₂O₃ device-oxide science and related power-electronics-relevant wide-bandgap semiconductors.

A Polish strategy oriented toward acquiring genuine bulk-growth know-how from Italy should treat **CNR-IMEM Parma and CNR-IMM Catania** as the primary targets, with Sapienza positioned as a valuable but secondary node.

## 5. Economic and Strategic Context for Poland

### 5.1 Why this distinction matters for Polish industrial policy

Poland has genuine, underexploited domestic assets in compound semiconductor bulk growth that predate and outclass anything documented at Sapienza:

- **Institute of Electronic Materials Technology (ITME, Warsaw)** — long-standing Polish capability in GaAs, GaN, and other compound semiconductor bulk/substrate technology.
- **Institute of High Pressure Physics, Polish Academy of Sciences (UNIPRESS)** — pioneer of high-nitrogen-pressure solution growth (HNPSG) for bulk GaN, a globally recognized Polish-originated technology (the "Porowski method"; note the direct citation of S. Porowski's foundational 1999 MRS work in the international bulk-GaN patent literature, confirming Poland's own priority position in this field).
- **Ammono S.A.** — Polish commercial spin-off producing ammonothermal bulk GaN crystals, one of only a handful of companies worldwide with this capability.

Given this, the strategically coherent narrative is **not** "Poland imports bulk-growth technology from Italy," but rather "Poland leverages and scales its own world-class bulk GaN heritage (UNIPRESS/Ammono, ITME) while importing complementary capabilities — device physics, characterization science, computational crystal-growth modelling, talent pipelines, and possibly wide-bandgap oxide (Ga₂O₃) expertise — from Italian partners including Sapienza, CNR-IMEM, and CNR-IMM."

### 5.2 Realistic value Sapienza brings to a Polish consortium

1. **Talent and joint doctoral training.** Sapienza's scale (3,000+ faculty, large PhD cohorts in Materials Science and Physics of Semiconductors) makes it a plausible partner for joint PhD programs, Marie Skłodowska-Curie actions, and staff exchange feeding a Polish wafer-fab ecosystem with trained personnel in semiconductor physics, defect characterization, and device design — even without bulk-growth-specific expertise.
2. **Advanced characterization services.** Sapienza's synchrotron-linked infrared/THz nanospectroscopy and electron/optical characterization capability could serve as a shared European characterization node for wafers and boules produced in Poland (defect mapping, strain analysis, optical quality assessment), complementing rather than competing with domestic Polish metrology.
3. **Computational and ab-initio materials modelling.** Sapienza's demonstrated ab-initio work on phase-change and chalcogenide heterostructures indicates a competency in first-principles modelling that could support materials selection and defect-engineering decisions in a wider Polish R&D program, alongside (not instead of) continuum melt-growth transport modelling, which is a separate discipline better sourced from institutions such as IKZ, Fraunhofer IISB, or specialized university groups (e.g., Derby/Minnesota, Dupret/UCLouvain lineage) referenced in the broader crystal-growth literature.
4. **EU funding architecture access.** As a large, EU-Horizon-active Italian university, Sapienza offers a credible consortium partner for Chips Joint Undertaking, Horizon Europe, and bilateral Italy–Poland science agreements, which matters for grant-writing and political visibility even where its direct technical contribution to bulk growth is limited.
5. **Adjacent device/application pull-through.** Sapienza's quantum photonics and phase-change/neuromorphic device work represents downstream *application demand* for high-quality compound semiconductor and oxide substrates — a useful "demand-side" academic partner validating the market case for a Polish wafer supply chain, distinct from being a supply-side technology source.

### 5.3 What Sapienza cannot substitute for

Poland should not expect from Sapienza:
- Transfer of operational bulk-growth process know-how (thermal-field design, crucible/susceptor engineering, dopant segregation control, dislocation/EPD reduction protocols) — this know-how, where it exists in Italy at all, sits at CNR-IMEM/CNR-IMM and is itself modest relative to German (IKZ, Fraunhofer IISB, Freiberg-based industry), Japanese, or US incumbents.
- Industrial-scale equipment, pilot lines, or a track record of licensing bulk-growth IP to industry.
- A pre-existing supply relationship with compound semiconductor device manufacturers analogous to CNR-IMM's relationship with STMicroelectronics' Catania SiC operations.

## 6. Recommendations

1. **Do not anchor the Polish ecosystem strategy on Sapienza for bulk-growth technology transfer.** Treat this as a science/talent/characterization partnership, not a process-technology partnership.
2. **Prioritize CNR-IMEM (Parma) and CNR-IMM (Catania)** as the Italian institutions with actual bulk-growth and wide-bandgap power-device-relevant experience, potentially alongside the University of Parma (Fornari's current base) for Ga₂O₃-specific collaboration.
3. **Build the domestic core around ITME, UNIPRESS/Ammono**, whose HNPSG/ammonothermal bulk GaN expertise is a genuinely world-class Polish asset that should be the technological spine of any new ecosystem, supplemented by targeted acquisition of PVT/sublimation SiC and AlN capability (an area where Poland has less domestic depth) from partners such as CNR-IMM, IKZ Berlin, or Fraunhofer IISB.
4. **Engage Sapienza specifically for:** joint doctoral training slots, access to synchrotron/advanced spectroscopic characterization for Polish-grown boules and wafers, ab-initio defect/materials modelling support, and co-authorship on Horizon Europe / Chips JU proposals where a large, high-visibility Italian university partner strengthens the consortium's profile.
5. **Commission a targeted technical due-diligence visit** to Sapienza's Physics Department and SBAI groups (De Luca, Polimeni, IRS lab) to confirm current instrumentation and willingness to commit characterization time/beamline access to Polish-origin material, before allocating any consortium funding lines to Sapienza specifically.
