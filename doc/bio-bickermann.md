# Scientific Biography of Matthias Bickermann

## Overview

Matthias Bickermann (born January 17, 1973, in Nuremberg, Germany) is a German materials scientist specializing in the growth and characterization of bulk single crystals, with particular expertise in wide-bandgap semiconductors, oxides, and fluorides. He holds a joint appointment as tenured Professor for "Fundamentals and Methods of Crystal Growth" at the Institute of Chemistry, Technische Universität Berlin, and as Deputy Director of the Leibniz-Institut für Kristallzüchtung (IKZ) Berlin, where he heads the Department "Volume Crystals" and leads its Section "Oxides and Fluorides." Over more than two decades he has become a central figure in the international crystal growth community, combining fundamental research on nucleation, defect formation, and growth–property correlations with the development of technologically relevant bulk crystals for electronic, optoelectronic, and laser applications.

## Education

- **1992–1998**: Studied Materials Science at the Friedrich-Alexander-University Erlangen-Nuremberg (FAU), graduating with a Diploma (equivalent to an M.Sci.) on November 28, 1998.
- **1998–2002**: Doctoral studies at the Chair of Electrical Engineering Materials, FAU Erlangen-Nuremberg, under advisor Prof. Dr. A. Winnacker. His dissertation, *"Growth and characterization of semi-insulating SiC bulk crystals"* (written in German), was defended on March 19, 2002.
- **2002–2008**: Habilitation in Materials Science at FAU Erlangen-Nuremberg, supervised by Prof. Dr. A. Winnacker, Prof. Dr. P. Schmuki, and Prof. Dr. A. Magerl. His habilitation thesis was titled *"Growth and characterization of AlN bulk crystals,"* and he was awarded the teaching qualification (venia docendi) on November 5, 2008.

## Research Focus

Bickermann's research centers on the correlation between crystal growth conditions and the resulting structural, electrical, and optical properties of bulk single crystals. His principal areas of interest include:

1. **Melt and flux growth of oxides and fluorides** — for optical, piezoelectric, and laser crystals, and as substrates for novel electronic devices.
2. **Wide-bandgap semiconductor bulk crystals** — growth and characterization for use as substrate materials in semiconductor technology, most notably aluminium nitride (AlN) and silicon carbide (SiC).
3. **General growth–property correlations** — including doping and defect formation during growth, and the homogeneity of structural, electrical, and optical properties in bulk crystals, studied through both experimental technique development and numerical process modeling.

At IKZ, the Department "Volume Crystals" under his leadership works with a broad materials portfolio spanning elementary semiconductors (Si, Ge), compound semiconductors (GaAs, InP), novel oxide semiconductors (notably β-Ga₂O₃), oxide substrates for functional thin films (e.g., strained ferroelectrics), and bulk optical, laser, fluoride, and piezo-/ferroelectric crystals, employing high-temperature melt growth techniques such as Czochralski, Floating Zone, Bridgman/Vertical Gradient Freeze (VGF), and Edge-defined Film-fed Growth (EFG).

Within the Section "Oxides and Fluorides," research groups under his direction pursue several thematic lines:

- **Fluoride Crystals for Photonic Applications** (Junior Research Group led by Dr. Hiroki Tanaka) — fluoride single crystals for laser materials, high-power optical isolators, nonlinear frequency conversion, deep-UV optical windows, and solid-state optical refrigeration.
- **Single Crystals of Oxide Semiconductors** (Dr. Zbigniew Galazka) — transparent conducting/semiconducting oxides, including pioneering work on 2-inch β-Ga₂O₃ single crystals grown by the Czochralski method, as well as In₂O₃, SnO₂, gallium-based spinels (MgGa₂O₄, ZnGa₂O₄, CoGa₂O₄), barium stannate (BaSnO₃), and lanthanum indate (LaInO₃).
- **Substrate Crystals for Advanced Functional Oxides** (Dr. Christo Guguschev) — bulk crystals with tailored lattice parameters (perovskite, pyrochlore, magnetoplumbite structures) as substrates for epitaxial oxide thin films with ferroelectric, superconducting, ferromagnetic, piezoelectric, multiferroic, or electron-transport properties.
- **Crystals for Benchmarking and Service** (Dr. Steffen Ganschow) — provision of reference and benchmark single crystals for academic, industrial, and technology-development partners.
- **Thermochemical and Phase Analysis** (Dr. Roberts Blukis) — thermochemical and phase-diagram analysis supporting the design of growth processes for materials with incongruent melting, decomposition, or oxidation-state instability.

He was previously responsible for IKZ's **Aluminium Nitride** activities (until 2019, when the group transferred to the Department "Application Science," subsequently led by Dr. Thomas Straubinger), reflecting his earlier doctoral and habilitation work on AlN and SiC bulk crystal growth by physical vapor transport (PVT).

He is also involved in the **Center for Laser Materials (ZLM)** at IKZ (led by PD Dr. Christian Kränkel), the **IKZ–Cornell Joint Lab** on oxide electronics with Prof. Darrell G. Schlom, the **Leibniz ScienceCampus GraFOx II** (focused on gallium oxide, strontium titanate, and high-electron-mobility oxides), and the **DFG Research Unit 5044** on periodic low-dimensional defect structures in lithium niobate–lithium tantalate solid solutions.

## Entrepreneurial Activity

In 2010, Bickermann co-founded the start-up company **CrystAl-N GmbH**, which worked to advance AlN growth technology and commercialize AlN substrates for UV optoelectronic applications. The company ceased operations in 2018.

## Publications and Intellectual Property

Bickermann has co-authored more than 140 peer-reviewed journal publications and 7 patents over the course of his career, spanning topics from SiC and AlN bulk crystal growth to oxide and fluoride single crystals, defect formation, and growth-process modeling. Representative recent contributions (2024–2025) include work on bulk single crystals of rutile GeO₂ for high-power electronics and deep-UV optoelectronics, lithium niobate–tantalate solid solutions, In₂O₃ single crystals grown by a levitation-assisted self-seeding method, and reviews of AlN and β-Ga₂O₃ crystal growth and epitaxy for next-generation power devices.

## Teaching and Academic Service

At Technische Universität Berlin, Bickermann delivers lectures at the Institute of Chemistry and supervises and reviews PhD, master's, and bachelor's theses at IKZ and TU Berlin. He has also served as external reviewer for dissertations and theses at the universities of Erlangen, Humboldt University Berlin, Cottbus, Dresden, Freiberg, Halle-Wittenberg, Ulm (Germany), and Warsaw (Poland). He organized the annual "IKZ Summer School on Crystal Growth" from 2012 to 2017 and leads the IKZ PhD seminar.

## Guiding Philosophy

Bickermann has described his institutional mission as seeking to "enable people and science" within the academic environment of IKZ, emphasizing the role of human expertise, mentorship, and the connection between fundamental understanding and applied technology development as the basis of sustained scientific and technological progress in crystal growth.
