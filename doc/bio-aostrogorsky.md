# Aleksandar G. Ostrogorsky — Scientific Biography

## Overview

Aleksandar G. Ostrogorsky is a professor in the Department of Mechanical, Materials, and Aerospace Engineering (MMAE), Armour College of Engineering, at the Illinois Institute of Technology (Illinois Tech), Chicago. He is a specialist in heat and mass transfer in materials processing, with a career-long focus on directional solidification and single-crystal growth of semiconductor and related functional materials, including work carried out aboard the International Space Station (ISS). He is a Fellow of the American Society of Mechanical Engineers (ASME) and an Associate Fellow of the American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics (AIAA).

## Academic Career

## Research Program

Ostrogorsky's research addresses **heat and mass transfer phenomena in materials processing**, with emphasis on **directional solidification and single-crystal growth**, spanning:

- **Directional solidification / Bridgman-type crystal growth** — theoretical, numerical (e.g., 3D simulation of melt–crystal interface shape), and experimental studies of convection, dopant segregation, and interface control during vertical Bridgman growth.
- **Submerged Heater Method (SHM)** — a crystal-growth process he invented in 1989 for producing higher-quality semiconductor crystals by submerging a heater within the melt to control convection and the melt–crystal interface.
- **Semiconductor single-crystal growth** — including doped germanium (Ge), gallium antimonide (GaSb), indium antimonide (InSb), indium phosphide (InP), and gallium-indium-antimonide (GaInSb) alloys for photovoltaic and thermophotovoltaic applications.
- **Wide-bandgap semiconductors and scintillators for gamma-ray/nuclear radiation detectors** — notably indium iodide (InI) and comparisons with materials such as cadmium zinc telluride (CZT), aiming to develop cheaper, faster-growing alternatives for radiation detection.
- **Piezoelectric crystal growth** — including relaxor-ferroelectric alloys such as PMN-PT (lead magnesium niobate–lead titanate, PMNx–PT1-x).
- **Growth of carbon nanotubes** — single-walled carbon nanotubes (SWNTs) via the electric-arc process.
- **Diffusion phenomena** in melt-growth systems.
- **Bioeffects of magnetic fields** — effects of magnetic fields on the physiology of living organisms at the molecular, cellular, tissue, and whole-body level (a more applied-biophysics strand of his broader transport-phenomena interests).

### Microgravity and Space-Based Research

A defining feature of Ostrogorsky's career is his sustained use of microgravity as an experimental tool to isolate and study buoyancy-driven convection during melt growth:

- **SUBSA (Solidification Using a Baffle in Sealed Ampoules), 2002** — As Principal Investigator, he led the first scientific investigation conducted in NASA's Microgravity Science Glovebox (MSG) aboard the ISS. The experiment grew tellurium- and zinc-doped indium antimonide (InSb) crystals from the melt, using specially designed furnace hardware with a transparent viewing section enabling real-time observation and precise measurement of growth rate and seeding.
- **CASIS/NASA-funded ISS investigation, "Detached Melt and Vapor Growth of InI in SUBSA Hardware" (funded ~2015)** — As PI, Ostrogorsky prepared and conducted a further series (eight) of crystal-growth experiments aboard the ISS, sponsored by the Center for the Advancement of Science in Space (CASIS) in conjunction with NASA. The goals were to (a) produce benchmark-quality indium iodide (InI) crystals — a candidate nuclear-radiation-detector semiconductor — and (b) develop an inexpensive terrestrial process for growing InI crystals, offering a potentially faster and cheaper alternative to the slow-growing (~5 mm/day), expensive CZT. He also served as a consultant on a related materials-science project selected in the same CASIS funding round.

## Scholarly Output

According to aggregated researcher-profile data (e.g., ResearchGate), Ostrogorsky's publication record includes on the order of **~79 tracked publications** with roughly **1,000+ citations**, concentrated in the areas of crystal growth, heat transfer, solidification, single-crystal materials, convection, fluid mechanics, and mass transfer. Representative research themes evident in his published work include:

- Thermophysical property characterization of indium(I) iodide crystals for detector applications.
- Comparative analyses of decades of space-based semiconductor crystal-growth experiments, synthesizing findings on the persistence of weak residual convection in microgravity melt-growth experiments, the defect-reducing role of de-wetting from ampoule walls, and the particular promise shown by vapor-phase growth experiments in space.

## Significance and Legacy

Ostrogorsky's career bridges classical thermal-fluid engineering and applied materials science, with a distinguishing emphasis on **using microgravity as a scientific instrument** — flying real crystal-growth hardware to the ISS not as a technology demonstration but to isolate diffusion-controlled transport from gravity-driven convection and thereby test and refine terrestrial process models (e.g., the Submerged Heater Method, baffle-controlled Bridgman growth). This has connected fundamental transport-phenomena research to practical downstream applications in:
- Infrared detectors and communications-grade semiconductors,
- Photovoltaic/thermophotovoltaic energy conversion,
- Piezoelectric actuator/sensor materials, and
- Compact, lower-cost nuclear-radiation (gamma-ray) detection materials as alternatives to CZT.

His long-running PI role on NASA/CASIS-sponsored ISS investigations (spanning from the 2002 SUBSA/MSG inaugural experiments to CASIS-funded InI growth work roughly a decade-plus later) situates him among a relatively small group of materials engineers with sustained, repeated access to ISS-based experimental facilities across multiple funding cycles.
