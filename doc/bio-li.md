## 4. Research programme

You-Rong Li's research can be organized into several long-running, mutually reinforcing threads. The throughline across nearly all of them is **buoyancy- and surface-tension-driven convection at deformable or evaporating interfaces**, studied through a combination of finite-difference/finite-volume numerical simulation, linear stability analysis, and — in his more recent work — carefully instrumented laboratory experiments and molecular dynamics simulation.

### 4.1 Thermocapillary and thermocapillary-buoyancy convection in melt pools (the core, longest-running line)

This is the dominant and most cited strand of his career, directly relevant to Czochralski (Cz) silicon crystal growth, where surface-tension gradients along a free melt surface drive convective flow that can become unstable and degrade crystal quality.

Representative sustained sub-themes:

- **Annular and cylindrical pool geometries.** A long sequence of papers (with collaborators including Nobuyuki Imaishi, Lan Peng, Shuang-Ying Wu, Dan-Ling Zeng, and later Li Zhang, Chun-Mei Wu, and Jia-Jia Yu) systematically mapped the onset of three-dimensional, oscillatory thermocapillary and thermocapillary-buoyancy flow in shallow and deep annular pools of silicon melt and of low-to-moderate Prandtl-number model fluids, establishing critical Marangoni/Reynolds numbers, critical wavenumbers, and transition routes (steady 2-D → 3-D steady → 3-D oscillatory, and further transitions toward chaos).
- **Czochralski (Cz) configuration modeling.** Extending the annular-pool work to the more realistic Cz configuration (with a central crystal/crucible geometry rather than a simple annulus), his group characterized how crystal rotation, pool depth, and the Cz-specific geometry alter flow stability compared to the idealized annular case.
- **Rotation and magnetic field effects.** Work with Wanyuan Shi, Michael K. Ermakov (Institute for Problems in Mechanics, Russian Academy of Sciences), and Imaishi examined the stabilizing/destabilizing role of pool rotation (including Coriolis effects, characterized via Taylor number) and, separately with Peng, Zhou, and Wen, the effect of cusp magnetic fields on flow structure during detached solidification of CdZnTe crystals.
- **Two-layer and binary-fluid systems.** Later work extended the single-fluid thermocapillary problem to two-layer immiscible systems (Li, Zhang, Wang, 2008) and to thermo-solutocapillary-buoyancy convection in binary fluids relevant to Cz growth of compound/doped crystals, including a 2025 paper on convection with a partly deformed free surface — indicating the thermocapillary research line has remained active for over two decades.
- **Surface heat dissipation and evaporative coupling.** A cluster of papers from 2018–2020 (with Li Zhang, Chun-Mei Wu, Qiu-Sheng Liu, Jin-Jing Li) examined how surface heat loss and, further, evaporative phase change at the free surface couple back into and modify thermocapillary-buoyancy flow patterns, bifurcation routes, and destabilization mechanisms — moving the research programme from idealized adiabatic free surfaces toward more physically complete boundary conditions.

### 4.2 Buoyancy convection near the density maximum of water (Rayleigh–Bénard and Rayleigh–Bénard–Marangoni)

A distinct but related thread examines natural and turbulent Rayleigh–Bénard convection of cold water near its density maximum (~4 °C), where water's non-monotonic density–temperature relationship produces convective behavior fundamentally different from convection in monotonic fluids. Work with Xiao-Jie Huang, Li Zhang, Yu-Peng Hu, and Lu Jia covered both cubical and cylindrical containers, turbulent regimes, and combined Rayleigh–Bénard–Marangoni (buoyancy plus surface-tension) configurations, as well as natural convection of water-based nanofluids near the density maximum in annular geometries — connecting the density-inversion physics to a nanofluid heat-transfer application.

### 4.3 Droplet evaporation and interfacial phase-change dynamics

From roughly 2019 onward, Li's group (notably with Shuang Ye, Qiu-Sheng Liu, Li Zhang, Rui-Feng Guo, Dong-Ming Mo, Chun-Mei Wu, and Yu Zhang) built an experimental and numerical programme on the evaporation dynamics of sessile droplets — first ethanol droplets on heated substrates, then droplets evaporating into their own pure vapor at reduced pressure — including direct temperature-field measurement near the evaporating interface in annular pools under radial temperature gradients at low pressure, and numerical analysis of the resulting flow instabilities. This line connects classical thermocapillary-convection expertise to the broader, industrially relevant problem of evaporative cooling and phase-change heat transfer.

### 4.4 Molecular-scale condensation, nucleation, and adsorption (recent expansion, ~2025)

The most recent identified work extends the group's interfacial-transport focus down to the molecular scale via molecular dynamics (MD) simulation:

- A 2025 *Physics of Fluids* paper (with Jun-Feng Shen, Chun-Mei Wu, Jia-Jia Yu) used MD simulations to examine interfacial-temperature-difference-driven water vapor diffusion through nitrogen gas and nucleation on nanostructured surfaces, comparing smooth and textured surfaces and proposing a theoretical prediction framework for the nucleation/condensation evolution.
- A 2025 *Industrial & Engineering Chemistry Research* paper integrated MD simulation with the Zeta adsorption isotherm to model liquid-phase formation and transport (pore filling and condensate evaporation) in nanoporous materials.

This indicates a deliberate broadening of the group's methodological toolkit from continuum CFD and linear stability analysis toward molecular-scale simulation, applied to problems (nucleation, wetting, nanoporous transport) that remain thematically continuous with the lab's long-standing interest in interfacial energy transport.

### 4.5 Thermal energy storage and applied thermal engineering

Alongside the fundamental fluid-physics programme, Li's group has published on more directly application-oriented thermal engineering problems, including melting-performance optimization and economic evaluation of rotating thermal energy storage systems using partially filled porous structures — reflecting his institutional research directions in "热能利用与节能技术" (thermal energy utilization and energy-conservation technology) and "新能源动力" (new-energy power).
