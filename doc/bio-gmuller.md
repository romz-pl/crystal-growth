# Georg Müller — Scientific Biography

**Founder of the Erlangen Crystal Growth Laboratory (CGL); Professor of Materials Science, Friedrich-Alexander-Universität Erlangen-Nürnberg; long-time Head of the Materials/Crystal Growth Department, Fraunhofer IISB**

---

## 1. Overview

Georg Müller is a German materials scientist and physicist recognized internationally as one of the foremost authorities on bulk crystal growth from the melt. Over a research career spanning more than three decades, he founded and led the **Erlangen Crystal Growth Laboratory (Kristalllabor, CGL)** — a joint research unit operated by the **Friedrich-Alexander-Universität Erlangen-Nürnberg (FAU)** and the **Fraunhofer Institute for Integrated Systems and Device Technology (IISB)** — building it into one of the world's leading centers for the experimental study and numerical simulation of crystal growth processes. His work bridges fundamental transport phenomena (melt convection, heat and mass transfer, segregation) with the industrial technologies used to produce semiconductor, optical, and metallurgical crystals.

## 4. The Erlangen Crystal Growth Laboratory (CGL)

Müller founded and directed the CGL, built on a legacy stretching back to Siemens' post-war semiconductor research at **Pretzfeld Castle** in Franconian Switzerland (birthplace of the "Siemens Process" for silicon purification) and to **Heinrich Welker's** discovery of compound semiconductor crystals at Siemens' Erlangen laboratories in the 1950s.

Under his leadership CGL became distinctive for:
- Operating as an **independent research unit** without an obligation to supply crystal material to other groups — a structural choice that allowed it to set its own research agenda rather than serve purely as a service laboratory.
- Combining **experimental crystal growth** with **numerical process simulation**, an integration that was unusual for its time and became a hallmark of the Erlangen approach.
- Sustained **international collaboration**, including a long-standing partnership with the crystal-growth group at the **West University of Timișoara**, Romania (initiated in 1995), through which roughly twenty Romanian scientists undertook research stays in Erlangen — several later becoming professors in Romania, joining German industry, or becoming Fraunhofer IISB staff. The collaboration also led to the founding of a Timișoara-based software company around 2000.
- **Space-based crystal growth research**: CGL participated in microgravity crystal-growth and solidification experiments beginning with the German Spacelab Mission D2 (1993) and continuing through subsequent space-based campaigns, including work related to materials processing aboard the International Space Station.
- Leadership of **MICAST** (Microstructure Formation in Casting of Technical Alloys under Diffusive and Convective Conditions), an international academic–industrial research project — headed for several years by Müller — exploiting microgravity to study diffusive transport during alloy solidification.

CGL's materials portfolio expanded over time from classical semiconductor crystals to a wide range of systems, including compound semiconductors (GaAs, GaSb, AlSb, InAs, Ge, InP, CdTe), multicrystalline and quasi-mono silicon for photovoltaics, optical and scintillator crystals (sapphire, CaF₂, LSO, YVO₄, Y₂O₃, CeBr₃), and metallic alloy solidification.

## 5. Research Contributions

Müller's scientific work centers on the physics of **melt growth** of crystals and its industrial control. Major threads include:

### 5.1 Melt convection and heat/mass transport
Foundational contributions to understanding **convective flow phenomena** during melt crystal growth — a critical factor governing dopant/impurity segregation, interface shape, and defect formation in grown crystals.

### 5.2 Vertical Gradient Freeze (VGF) technology
Development and advancement of the **VGF growth technique**, an important melt-growth method for compound semiconductor bulk crystals, contributing to its establishment as an industrially viable process.

### 5.3 Numerical process simulation
Pioneering the use of **global numerical modeling** for industrial crystal growth furnaces, including heat transfer, melt/crystal interface shape prediction, species transport (O, N, C), and precipitate formation (SiO₂, Si₃N₄, SiC) during silicon crystallization. This work underpinned simulation software developed and commercialized by Fraunhofer IISB, notably:
- **CrysVUn**
- **STHAMAS**
- **CrysMAS**

These tools were licensed worldwide to industry, extending the impact of CGL's research directly into commercial crystal growth equipment design and process optimization.

### 5.4 Silicon for photovoltaics and microelectronics
Later-career research addressed directional solidification of **multicrystalline and quasi-mono silicon ingots**, including modeling of impurity incorporation (iron contamination, seed-crystal effects) and their influence on minority-carrier lifetime distribution — work directly relevant to solar cell efficiency.

### 5.5 Alloy solidification and microgravity science
Research into the **solidification microstructure of metallic alloys** (e.g., directionally solidified Al–Si alloys), including the influence of applied rotating magnetic fields, combining experiment with multi-scale dendritic microstructure simulation (building on Wang & Beckermann's framework) implemented in CrysMAS.

### 5.6 Optical and functional crystals
Contributions to growth process control for optical-grade crystals such as **calcium fluoride (CaF₂)**, recognized with the German Stifterverband Science Prize (see Honors).

## 8. Post-Retirement Activity

Since retiring from his university chair on 31 March 2007, Müller has continued to be active in the field as an **independent consultant** through his firm **Crystal Consulting**, advising on crystal growth technology, and has remained connected to the Erlangen crystal-growth community — for example, being honored at the 4th Kristalllabor Symposium (20 January 2012) marking his 70th birthday, which reviewed his career and scientific legacy.

---

## 9. Legacy

Georg Müller's career is closely identified with establishing **Erlangen** as one of the pre-eminent European centers for crystal growth research — a status the CGL/Fraunhofer IISB Materials Department has sustained for four decades. His distinctive combination of hands-on experimental crystal growth, first-principles transport modeling, and industrially deployed simulation software created a template later continued by his successors (notably Jochen Friedrich) at Fraunhofer IISB, and his mentorship — including sustained international training partnerships such as the Erlangen–Timișoara collaboration — helped seed crystal-growth research groups and industrial careers across Europe.
