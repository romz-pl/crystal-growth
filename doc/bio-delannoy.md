## 1. Overview

Yves Delannoy is a French professor of fluid mechanics and process engineering at Grenoble INP – Phelma, attached to the SIMaP laboratory's EPM (Élaboration par Procédés Magnétiques) group. Over a career spanning more than three decades, his work has moved from aeronautical and hydraulic cavitation research to high-temperature process engineering, with magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) as the unifying methodological thread. He is recognized internationally for combining numerical simulation (finite-volume CFD, custom MHD solvers) with industrial process development — most notably in the purification of metallurgical/solar-grade silicon by plasma and cold-gas routes, in induction melting and crystal growth, and in electromagnetic pumping and stirring of liquid metals.

## 2. Education and Training

- **1985** — Graduated in Aeronautics from Sup'Aéro (École Nationale Supérieure de l'Aéronautique et de l'Espace), Toulouse.
- **1989** — Master's degree and PhD in Mechanics, Grenoble (Institut National Polytechnique de Grenoble). His doctoral research addressed two-phase/cavitating flow modelling, an area he continued to publish in through the early 1990s (e.g., unsteady cavitation modelling in turbopump inducers, work later cited in cavitation CFD literature such as Coutier-Delgosha, Reboud & Delannoy, 2003, *International Journal for Numerical Methods in Fluids*).
- **2005** — Habilitation à Diriger des Recherches (HDR) — accreditation to supervise doctoral research — in Materials and Process Engineering, Grenoble.

Before settling into the SIMaP/EPM group, Delannoy's early career (1990–1994) was spent in applied hydraulic and aerospace propulsion research — first at CREMHyG, a Grenoble hydraulic-machinery test center, then in industry at Société Européenne de Propulsion, where cavitation instabilities in cryogenic turbopump inducers (relevant to rocket engines such as those developed for Ariane launchers) were a central concern. This period produced foundational work on two-phase cavitating-flow models later used as reference points by other researchers modelling unsteady cavitation in turbopump blade cascades.

His return to academia as Assistant Professor in 1995 shifted his focus toward magnetohydrodynamics and high-temperature materials processing, culminating in his promotion to full Professor in 2007 within SIMaP's EPM group — one of the leading French groups in electromagnetic processing of liquid metals and semiconductor melts.

## 4. Research Domains

According to his own institutional profile, Delannoy's teaching and research span:

- **Fluid mechanics**
- **Heat and mass transfer**
- **Numerical methods** (primarily finite-volume CFD)
- **Magnetohydrodynamics (MHD)** — flow and transfer phenomena in the presence of static or oscillating/travelling magnetic fields
- **Elaboration (materials processing) using electromagnetic means** — purification of solar-grade silicon via inductive thermal plasmas, and melting/crystal growth in induction furnaces

### 4.1 Cavitation and Two-Phase Flow (early career, ~1989–2003)
His doctoral and immediate post-doctoral work established numerical approaches to unsteady cavitating flows, including a two-phase-flow modelling approach for cavitation (Delannoy & Kueny, 1990, *Cavitation and Multiphase Flow Forum*, ASME-FED). This methodology was subsequently extended and cited in later unsteady cavitation studies of turbopump inducer cascades (e.g., Coutier-Delgosha, Reboud & Delannoy, 2003), reflecting continuing relevance of his early modelling framework to aerospace turbomachinery research.

### 4.2 Magnetohydrodynamics (MHD)
A core and continuing research theme, covering:
- **Electromagnetic (induction) pumps** for liquid-metal handling with no moving parts or electrodes, including large-scale travelling-magnetic-field pumps developed for liquid sodium in fast-neutron nuclear reactors, and associated MHD instabilities arising from field convection by the flow.
- **MHD channel flow in inhomogeneous magnetic fields**, including numerical modelling contributions (with F. Dubois, J. Etay, O. Widlund) validated against the Ilmenau "Galinstan" experimental liquid-metal loop, using a custom in-house MHD module coupled to the commercial CFD solver Fluent.
- **Continuous casting** of metals, comparing numerical simulation against experimental measurement of electromagnetically influenced flow (with O. Widlund, J. Etay).

### 4.3 High-Temperature Process Engineering: Silicon Purification for Photovoltaics
This is arguably the most industrially visible strand of his output, situated within France's push (notably the **PHOTOSIL** project, a consortium of FerroPEM, CEA-INES, CNRS/SIMaP and Apollon Solar) to develop cost-competitive solar-grade silicon via metallurgical (non-Siemens) purification routes:
- **Plasma torch and inductive plasma purification** of metallurgical-grade silicon, using an inductively stirred liquid-silicon free surface exposed to a plasma jet to accelerate impurity transfer from bulk liquid to surface (with C. Alemany, K.-I. Li, C. Trassy).
- **Modelling of inductively coupled plasmas** at atmospheric pressure, including work examining the limits of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) assumptions near walls and plasma-impacted surfaces (with J. Lacombe, C. Trassy).
- **CFD modelling of boron removal** from liquid silicon using cold reactive gases (Ar–H₂–H₂O) and thermal plasma (Ar–H₂–O₂) routes, including reasoning about aerosol nucleation effects on mass transfer (with M. Vadon, Ø. Sortland, I. Nuta, C. Chatillon, M. Tangstad, G. Chichignoud).
- Broader review work synthesizing production routes and impurity specifications for undoped, compensated, and heavily compensated solar-grade silicon (2012 review article).
- Continued supervision of purification research into wide-area reactor configurations (e.g., PhD of Hamza Abouchi, defended 2020, co-supervised with Guy Chichignoud).

### 4.4 Crystal Growth and Solidification under Magnetic Fields
Work on the influence of fluid flow on solute segregation during crystallization, spanning:
- Diffusive regimes obtained by suppressing convection with strong magnetic fields (relevant to electronic-grade crystal growth, avoiding striations and dopant-level fluctuations).
- Turbulent forced convection regimes deliberately used to enhance segregation/purification effects in solar-silicon solidification (work presented with K. Zaidat at EMRS).
- Supervision and examination of related PhD theses on Bridgman solidification of semiconductor alloys under external fields (e.g., Carmen Stelian, co-supervised with Thierry Duffar, defended 2005) and on segregation modelling for photovoltaic-grade silicon directional solidification (external jury/rapporteur roles, e.g., for theses at LMFA Lyon).

Delannoy is a permanent member of SIMaP's **EPM (Élaboration par Procédés Magnétiques)** team, one of SIMaP's constituent research groups (alongside Génie Physique et Mécanique des Matériaux, Physique du Métal, and Thermodynamique/Modélisation/Optimisation des Procédés). The EPM group specializes in electromagnetic processing of liquid metals and semiconductors — induction melting, electromagnetic stirring/pumping/levitation, and MHD control of free surfaces and interfaces during solidification — and Delannoy's numerical-modelling expertise in fluid flow and heat/mass transfer has been a methodological backbone for the group's experimental and industrial-process work.

## 9. Summary Assessment

Yves Delannoy's scientific trajectory illustrates a coherent methodological continuity — numerically grounded fluid-dynamics modelling — applied across strikingly different physical regimes: from cavitating cryogenic turbopump flows in aerospace propulsion, to electromagnetically driven liquid-metal and plasma flows in materials elaboration. His most sustained and industrially consequential contribution has been to the modelling and process design of silicon purification for photovoltaics, situating fundamental transport-phenomena modelling directly within a French national effort (PHOTOSIL) to establish cost-competitive, lower-energy alternatives to conventional Siemens-process solar silicon. His role as long-serving thesis supervisor and external examiner has additionally propagated this modelling methodology to a wider community working on solidification, segregation, and MHD process control in materials science.
