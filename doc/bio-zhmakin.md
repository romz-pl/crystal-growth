# Alexander Igorevich Zhmakin: An Exhaustive Scientific Biography

## Overview

Alexander Igorevich Zhmakin (Александр Игоревич Жмакин) was a Russian computational physicist who spent his entire scientific career at the Ioffe Institute (A.F. Ioffe Physico-Technical Institute) of the Russian Academy of Sciences in St. Petersburg, where he rose to the position of Leading Researcher in the Computational Physics Laboratory (later the Numerical Simulation Department). Over five decades he built a distinctive body of work spanning computational fluid dynamics, crystal growth modeling, semiconductor device and nanostructure simulation, cryobiology, human motion simulation, and — in his final years — non-classical (non-Fourier) heat transfer theory, culminating in a trilogy of research monographs published by Springer.

## Personal Details and Education

- **Born:** March 12, 1951, in Leningrad, USSR (one Springer author note gives December 3, 1951; the majority of sources, including his most recent monograph, state March 12, 1951).
- **Education:** Graduated from the Leningrad Polytechnic Institute in 1974.
- **Candidate of Physical-Mathematical Sciences (Ph.D. equivalent), 1980** — thesis on the numerical simulation of non-equilibrium shocked flows, defended at the Leningrad Polytechnical Institute.
- **Doctor of Physical-Mathematical Sciences (Dr. Sci.), 1992** — for research on the numerical simulation of gas- and liquid-phase epitaxial crystal growth.

## Institutional Career

Zhmakin joined the Ioffe Institute immediately upon graduating in 1974 and remained affiliated with it for his entire career, ultimately serving as **Leading Researcher of the Computational Physics Laboratory** (variously listed in his later publications under the Institute's Numerical Simulation Department). He was also associated with the **St. Petersburg Branch of the Joint Supercomputing Center (JSCC) of the Russian Academy of Sciences**.

Alongside his academic post, Zhmakin maintained a long industrial collaboration with **Soft-Impact Ltd.**, a St. Petersburg-based company providing consulting services and developing simulation software for crystal growth processes and semiconductor device modeling:

- **Technical Director**, 1997–2003
- **Research Director**, 2004–2008
- **Scientific Consultant**, 2009 onward

Through Soft-Impact, Zhmakin oversaw much of the architecture and development of industrially deployed simulation software, bridging academic modeling with applied engineering practice for crystal-growth and semiconductor manufacturers. He also mentored numerous undergraduate and graduate students, researchers, and engineers over the course of his career.

## Evolution of Research Interests

Zhmakin's scientific trajectory moved through several distinct but connected phases, each reflected in a substantial published output.

### 1. Computational Fluid Dynamics and Shock Physics (1970s–early 1980s)
His earliest work, forming the basis of his Candidate thesis, addressed computational fluid dynamics of **supersonic magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flows** and problems of **shock-wave interaction with obstacles** — numerically demanding, non-equilibrium gas-dynamics problems characteristic of Soviet-era aerospace and plasma physics research.

### 2. Viscous Flows, Heat Transfer, and Crystal Growth (mid-1980s–1990s)
From the mid-1980s his interests shifted toward **low-Mach-number viscous gas flows and heat transfer**, and subsequently toward the numerical simulation of **crystal growth processes**. He developed and applied simulation algorithms for:
- Gas-phase, liquid-phase, and vapor-phase epitaxy
- Bulk and sublimation crystal growth methods
- Coupled fluid dynamics and heat transfer in growth reactors

This period also produced several co-authored papers on **fluid flows and crystal growth under microgravity conditions**, connecting his numerical work to space-based materials-processing research. This body of work underpinned his 1992 Dr. Sci. dissertation.

### 3. Industrial Simulation and Semiconductor Modeling (1990s–2000s)
Beginning in the 1990s, and accelerating through his role at Soft-Impact, Zhmakin's research increasingly involved **industrial collaboration** — translating academic crystal-growth models into simulation tools used in commercial reactor design (e.g., for SiC and AlN bulk crystal and epitaxial growth). In the 2000s his scope broadened to include:
- **Semiconductor nanostructures**, including III–V (A3B5) compound semiconductor structure modeling
- **Light-emitting diodes (LEDs)** based on complex heterostructures, including coupled modeling of current spreading, thermal effects, and light extraction in III-nitride LEDs
- Radiative heat transfer and global heat/mass/oxygen transport modeling in melt-growth systems (e.g., bismuth germanate crystal growth)

### 4. Interdisciplinary Excursions: Human Motion and Cryobiology
Zhmakin pursued two significant interdisciplinary research threads outside core semiconductor/crystal physics:

- **Cryobiology.** His comprehensive monograph *Fundamentals of Cryobiology: Physical Phenomena and Mathematical Models* (Springer, 2008/2009) surveyed the physical phenomena and mathematical/numerical models common to cryosurgery and cryopreservation, treating the field from the physicist's perspective while remaining accessible to biologists and clinicians.
- **Human motion modeling.** First presented in a 2011 article, this interest matured into the monograph *Human Motion: From Animation to Simulation — A View from the Outside* (LAP LAMBERT Academic Publishing, 2021), which examined computer-animation and biomechanical simulation approaches to human movement from a physicist/outsider's vantage point.

### 5. Non-Classical Heat Transfer (2020s, final research phase)
In his last years Zhmakin concentrated on **non-Fourier heat conduction** — heat transport regimes where the classical Fourier law breaks down (ultrafast heating, nanoscale transport, granular/porous media, and biological tissue). This culminated in two closely linked monographs:
- *Non-Fourier Heat Conduction: From Phase-Lag Models to Relativistic and Quantum Transport* (Springer, 2023), covering phase-lag, phonon, and thermomass models, relativistic and quantum transport (including Landauer-formalism and Green–Kubo linear-response approaches), and an appendix on fractional calculus methods.
- *Heat Transfer in Vivo* (Springer Series in Biophysics, vol. 27, 2025), which extended non-Fourier and classical (Pennes bioheat) frameworks to a comprehensive treatment of thermal physics in living organisms — from whole-body thermoregulation down to cellular and sub-cellular heat transfer, haemodynamics, regional (skin and deep-tissue) models, hyperthermia, cryomedicine, and thermal ablation.

Colleagues noted that Zhmakin's books were distinguished by "their precise and thoughtful selection of epigraphs" — a recognized stylistic hallmark of his scientific writing.
