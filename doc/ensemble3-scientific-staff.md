# ENSEMBLE3 Centre of Excellence: A Technical and Scientific Report on Its Scientific Staff

## 1. Institutional Overview

ENSEMBLE3 (formally: *Centre of Excellence for nanophotonics, advanced materials and novel crystal growth-based technologies*, styled "e3") is a Warsaw-based International Research Agenda ("Teaming for Excellence") Centre of Excellence, established jointly by institutions from Poland, Germany, Italy, and Spain. The Centre is funded through the International Research Agendas Programme (IRAP) of the Foundation for Polish Science (FNP), co-financed by the EU under axis IV of the Smart Growth Operational Programme, Measure 4.3, together with the H2020 "Teaming for Excellence" programme (Grant Agreement No. 857543).

The Centre builds on two legacy pillars in Warsaw: (i) nanophotonics- and medicine-oriented research at the **University of Warsaw (UW)**, and (ii) crystal-growth research and development traditionally carried out at the **Łukasiewicz Research Network – Institute of Microelectronics and Photonics (IMiF)**, formerly the Institute of Electronic Materials Technology (ITME). The scientific programme explicitly continues the legacy of **Prof. Jan Czochralski**, inventor of the eponymous crystal-growth method that remains the dominant industrial technique for growing single crystals of silicon, germanium, and related compound semiconductors.

By the end of its third reporting period, the Centre reported a headcount of 95 people, comprising 44 scientists, 28 laboratory employees, and 23 support staff.

ENSEMBLE3 is organized as a limited-liability company, **Ensemble3 sp. z o.o.** (KRS 0000858669), headquartered at 133 Wólczyńska St., 01-919 Warsaw (Młociny district), and operates under the leadership of its President of the Management Board.

## 6. Scientific Scope and Interdisciplinarity

Collectively, ENSEMBLE3's scientific staff span:
- **Crystal growth and materials technology**: Czochralski, Bridgman, floating-zone/TSFZ, flux, and directional-solidification (eutectic) methods for oxide, semiconductor, and composite single crystals.
- **Nanophotonics and plasmonics**: metamaterials, plasmonic nanoantennas, topological photonics, terahertz optoelectronics.
- **Theoretical and computational condensed-matter physics**: 2D materials/heterostructures, inverse materials design, Casimir/Casimir–Polder physics, diffusion kinetics in nanosystems.
- **Energy applications**: photoelectrochemistry, water-splitting, up-conversion photovoltaics, scintillators for medical imaging (PET) and radiation detection.
- **Device characterization**: electrical, optical, and electromagnetic property measurement of wafers, transistors, sensors, and resonators.

This breadth reflects the Centre's design goal — bridging fundamental crystal-growth science (the historical strength of Polish materials science, per the Czochralski legacy) with cutting-edge nanophotonics and materials theory, in service of applications in telecommunications, solar energy, medicine, security, and aerospace.
