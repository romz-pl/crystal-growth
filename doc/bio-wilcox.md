# William R. Wilcox — Scientific Biography

**Distinguished University Professor Emeritus, Department of Chemical & Biomolecular Engineering, Clarkson University**

## Overview

William R. Wilcox (1935–2020) was a chemical engineer and materials scientist internationally recognized for his work on crystal growth, particularly the influence of gravity, convection, and processing conditions on the growth of semiconductor and optical crystals. He spent 45 years at Clarkson University in Potsdam, New York, where he served as chair of the chemical engineering department, dean of engineering for a decade, and founding director of two major research centers: the New York State Center for Advanced Materials Processing (CAMP) and NASA's Consortium for Commercial Crystal Growth in Space. His association with NASA's microgravity materials research program spanned roughly three decades, including ground-based studies and flight experiments aboard Skylab, the Space Shuttle, and sounding rockets.

As Dean of Engineering, Wilcox oversaw the creation of a B.S. program in aeronautical engineering (securing Clarkson, New York State Department of Education, and accreditation approval) and the establishment of doctoral programs in civil, electrical, and mechanical engineering. Near the end of his deanship he secured National Science Foundation funding to relocate the engineering school into the CAMP building.

## Research Focus

Wilcox's research centered on **crystal growth** and **materials processing**, with recurring themes including:

- Influence of gravity level (normal, micro-, and hyper-gravity via centrifugation) on convection and resulting crystal quality
- Directional solidification of semiconductor and eutectic alloys (e.g., InSb, Si–Ge, Al–Si–Ge systems)
- Impurity segregation and distribution during crystal growth
- Fibrous eutectic microstructure control via convection
- Phase-field modeling of two-dimensional phase-transition problems, using Fourier collocation and Fourier Galerkin numerical methods
- High-gravity (centrifuge) materials processing — he was associated with Clarkson's International Center for Gravity Materials Science and Applications and its "HIRB" (High Inertia Rotating Behemoth) centrifuge facility, the only one in the world dedicated solely to materials-processing research and flow visualization
- Commercial and space-based crystal growth, including flight experiments on Skylab, Space Shuttle missions, and sounding rockets

## Legacy

Wilcox is remembered at Clarkson as a formative figure who built both the university's chemical engineering department and its broader engineering school infrastructure, while sustaining a productive, multi-decade research program linking earth-based and space-based crystal growth science. His NASA-era microgravity work — spanning Skylab through Space Shuttle missions — situates him among a relatively small cohort of engineers whose laboratory research was directly validated through spaceflight experimentation. His institution-building (CAMP, the Consortium for Commercial Crystal Growth in Space, and the engineering school's move into the CAMP building) left a lasting physical and organizational imprint on Clarkson's engineering programs.
