# Takao Tsukada (塚田隆夫) — Scientific Biography

## Overview

Takao Tsukada (塚田 隆夫), often referred to in English-language literature as "Takao Tsukada" and closely associated with the transliteration "Taketoshi Tsukada" in some indexing contexts, is a Japanese chemical engineer who spent his entire academic career at **Tohoku University** in Sendai, Japan. He rose from research assistant to full professor within the **Department of Chemical Engineering, Graduate School of Engineering**, where he led the Process/Materials Control Process Engineering laboratory (物質制御プロセス工学分野) within the Process Systems Engineering division. His research career centers on the numerical simulation and experimental characterization of transport phenomena — heat, mass, and momentum transfer — in materials processing systems, with particular depth in crystal growth, high-temperature melt behavior under microgravity and electromagnetic levitation, and, in later years, nanoparticle interfacial phenomena and supercritical hydrothermal synthesis.

Note on naming: this biography treats "Taketoshi (Takao) Tsukada" as referring to **Takao Tsukada** of Tohoku University's Department of Chemical Engineering, the individual whose extensive, continuous publication record (from the late 1990s through 2026) matches the "Tohoku University" affiliation specified in the request. He should not be confused with (a) **Takeshi Tsukada**, a computer scientist and programming-languages researcher currently at Chiba University (formerly University of Tokyo), or (b) **Taketoshi Hibiya**, a separate materials scientist who frequently co-authored papers with Takao Tsukada on levitation and thermophysical property measurement.

## Education and Early Career

- Tsukada undertook his graduate education at **Tohoku University's Graduate School of Engineering**, in the Department of Chemical Engineering.
- He completed his doctoral studies there, receiving his **Doctor of Engineering (工学博士)** degree from Tohoku University in **1984**, following completion of the three-year doctoral program in the Chemical Engineering major.
- His early research life was spent entirely within the Tohoku University chemical engineering community, first as a research assistant (助手), then lecturer (講師), then associate professor (助教授), before a period as a professor at **Osaka Prefecture University**, and his eventual return to Tohoku University.

## Research Program

Tsukada's body of work — spanning well over 150 peer-reviewed journal articles and numerous conference proceedings, invited chapters, and technical reviews — can be organized into several major, interlocking research threads.

### 1. Numerical Simulation of Crystal Growth Processes
A recurring theme throughout Tsukada's career is the **coupled heat-transfer and fluid-flow analysis of single-crystal growth**, particularly by the **Czochralski (CZ) method**, historically the dominant industrial technique for growing silicon and oxide single crystals for the semiconductor and optical industries. His work in this area includes:
- Global heat-transfer models of the CZ furnace that account for **radiative heat transfer**, including **specular reflection at the crystal surface**, using radiation element methods (e.g., the REM2 method) to handle semitransparent single crystals.
- Studies quantifying the effect of **radiation heat shields** on silicon single-crystal growth by the CZ method (recognized with a **Society of Chemical Engineers, Japan (SCEJ) Paper Award**).
- Three-dimensional, time-dependent modeling of melt convection, solid–liquid interface shape, and dopant/impurity distribution in industrially relevant crystal-growth reactors.

### 2. Microgravity and Space-Based Crystal Growth
Tsukada was a long-term participant in Japan's microgravity materials-science research program, collaborating with JAXA-affiliated researchers on experiments conducted aboard the **International Space Station (ISS)**:
- Numerical and experimental studies of **SiGe (silicon-germanium) crystal growth via the Traveling Liquidus-Zone (TLZ) method**, examining the growth interface shape and compositional (Ge/Si) distribution under microgravity conditions, where buoyancy-driven convection is suppressed.
- Investigation of how temperature gradients affect Si₀.₅Ge₀.₅ crystal growth aboard the ISS (published in *Journal of Crystal Growth*), and later work (through 2026) on **increasing SiGe crystal growth rates using microgravity** (published in *Materialia*), indicating this remained an active line of inquiry into his later career.
- Numerical evaluation of **droplet-oscillation techniques** for measuring the interfacial (surface) tension between molten slag and molten iron aboard the ISS.

### 3. Electromagnetic Levitation and High-Temperature Thermophysical Properties
In close, long-running collaboration with Prof. Hiroyuki Fukuyama and others at Tohoku University's Institute of Multidisciplinary Research for Advanced Materials (IMRAM), Tsukada contributed extensively to **containerless processing** research using **electromagnetic levitators (EML)**, often superimposed with a **static magnetic field** to suppress internal convection and surface oscillation in molten metal droplets:
- Precision measurement (and underlying numerical/theoretical analysis) of **thermal conductivity, specific heat, normal spectral emissivity, density, and surface tension** of high-temperature melts (e.g., molten copper, molten Cu–Co and Cu–Fe alloys).
- Numerical simulation of **magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) convection**, including **laminar-turbulent transition**, inside electromagnetically levitated molten droplets under applied static magnetic fields.
- Investigation of **phase-separation structures** in undercooled Cu–Co and Cu–Fe alloys, including neutron computed tomography (CT) studies correlating three-dimensional phase-separation morphology with melt convection during solidification (published in *Scripta Materialia*).
- This work situated Tsukada within Japan's broader **THERMOLAB**-related research effort, generating reference thermophysical data for materials-science experiments conducted on the ISS.

### 4. Nanoparticle Interfacial Science and Polymer Nanocomposites
From roughly the mid-2010s onward, Tsukada's group (working closely with Prof. Masaki Kubo) shifted increasing emphasis toward the **interfacial structure, dispersion, and aggregation behavior of surface-modified (organic-ligand-functionalized) nanoparticles**, motivated by applications in nanocomposite materials and thermal interface materials for electronics:
- **Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations** of the interfacial structure, work of adhesion, and interfacial thermal resistance between surface-modified metal oxide nanoparticles (e.g., Al₂O₃) and polymers or organic solvents.
- Numerical (Brownian dynamics / DEM-style) simulation of **dispersion and aggregation of surface-modified nanoparticles under shear flow**, relevant to nanocomposite processing.
- Studies of **dewetting behavior and crack formation** in polymer nanocomposite thin films containing organic-modified nanoparticles, and of the spatial/structural organization of nanoparticles within such films during solvent casting and annealing.
- Later collaborative papers (2022–2025) extending this line into wetting phenomena of nanofluid droplets and interfacial-affinity evaluation via all-atom MD simulation, work that continued to credit Tsukada as a contributing/senior co-author even as the laboratory's day-to-day leadership passed to Prof. Kubo.

### 5. Supercritical Hydrothermal Synthesis and Reactor Engineering
Tsukada also contributed to research on **supercritical water/hydrothermal synthesis of nanoparticles**, an area strongly associated with Tohoku University's broader strength in this field (e.g., work connected to Prof. Tadafumi Adschiri's group):
- Numerical analysis of flow and thermal fields in **flow-type supercritical hydrothermal reactors/mixers**, relating reactor and mixer geometry to the size distribution of synthesized nanoparticles.
- Use of **neutron radiography and neutron computed tomography** as in-situ diagnostic tools to visualize reactant-solution behavior (e.g., heavy-oil flow in packed beds, "bumping" behavior during batch hydrothermal synthesis) inside opaque, high-pressure reactor vessels — reflecting a broader methodological interest in coupling advanced imaging diagnostics with numerical transport modeling.

### 6. Other Transport-Phenomena Studies
Earlier and parallel strands of Tsukada's published work include:
- Phase-separation dynamics in binary-mixture fluid layers subjected to combined thermal and concentration gradients.
- Photothermal poling of nonlinear-optical polymer films and photoacoustic measurement of thermal diffusivity in polymer thin films.
- Aggregation behavior of colloidal (latex) particles under confined shear flow.
- Contributions to thermophysical-property measurement techniques more broadly (e.g., ionic-liquid viscosity and thermal conductivity at elevated pressure).

## Legacy and Succession

By the early-to-mid 2020s, day-to-day leadership of Tsukada's laboratory at Tohoku University's Department of Chemical Engineering had transitioned to **Professor Masaki Kubo**, his long-time collaborator, with the group's official web presence being maintained under Kubo's name from that point forward. Tsukada nonetheless continued to appear as a co-author on the group's papers into 2026 (e.g., the *Materialia* paper on SiGe crystal growth in microgravity), indicating a continuing, if reduced, involvement in the research program he had built over four decades — spanning classical Czochralski crystal-growth heat transfer, space-based and levitation-based materials processing, and, most recently, nanoscale interfacial engineering for advanced composite materials.
