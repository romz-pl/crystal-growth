# Thierry Duffar — Scientific Biography

**Professeur Émérite, SIMaP (Science et Ingénierie des Matériaux et des Procédés), Université Grenoble Alpes / Grenoble INP, France**

---

## 1. Overview

Thierry Duffar is a French materials scientist and chemical engineer whose career has centered on the physics and engineering of **crystal growth from the melt**, with particular emphasis on **capillary phenomena, crucible/melt interactions (wetting and dewetting), microgravity crystal growth, electromagnetic processing, defect engineering, and the directional solidification of semiconductor and oxide crystals** (notably InSb, GaSb, CdTe/CdZnTe, sapphire and Ti:sapphire, and photovoltaic silicon). Over more than four decades he combined a strong experimental practice — including numerous space-flown crystal growth experiments conducted with CNES and ESA — with process modelling and numerical simulation, and became one of the internationally recognized authorities on the role of capillarity and wetting in melt-growth techniques (Bridgman, Czochralski, Floating Zone, shaped-crystal growth).

He spent his career largely at Grenoble, first as a researcher at the French Commissariat à l'Énergie Atomique (CEA), then as professor at Institut National Polytechnique de Grenoble (Grenoble INP), within the SIMaP laboratory (and its predecessor EPM — Élaboration par Procédés Magnétiques — group), before being appointed Professeur Émérite.

## 4. Research Themes

Duffar's own summary of his research areas (SIMaP laboratory record) groups his work as:

- **Study of all crystallogenesis (crystal growth) processes, and photovoltaic silicon** — covering essentially the full span of melt-growth techniques (Bridgman/vertical Bridgman, Czochralski, Verneuil, shaped-crystal/EFG-type growth, directional solidification) as applied both to compound semiconductors and to multicrystalline/directionally-solidified silicon for photovoltaics.
- **Numerical simulation** — of heat and mass transfer, interface shape, capillary/meniscus behaviour, segregation, and defect formation during melt growth.
- **Experiments in microgravity and under magnetic field** — a defining strand of his career, using orbital and sub-orbital microgravity platforms together with electromagnetic (magnetic field / electromagnetic processing) control of melts on the ground.

### 4.1 Capillarity, wetting and dewetting in crystal growth

A central and internationally recognized contribution is his systematic study of **capillary phenomena at the crystal–melt–crucible triple line**, including:
- **Growth angles** and their role in controlling crystal shape stability (Czochralski, EFG, shaped growth, Verneuil).
- **Wetting and dewetting of the melt on the crucible wall** during vertical Bridgman growth of semiconductors — the phenomenon by which the growing crystal detaches from the ampoule/crucible wall, first observed spontaneously in microgravity (Skylab InSb experiments) and subsequently studied and controlled by Duffar and collaborators both in space and, via applied gas-pressure differentials, on Earth ("dewetted Bridgman" / detached solidification).
- Theoretical and experimental treatment of **meniscus shape**, the pressure difference needed to sustain a stable detached growth configuration, and how atmosphere composition (oxidizing vs. non-oxidizing) governs whether stable dewetting is achieved (e.g., contrasting behaviour of GaSb and InSb).

This body of work underlies his editorship of the reference volume on capillarity in crystal growth (Section 6).

### 4.2 Microgravity crystal growth

Duffar has been a long-standing investigator in **space-based crystal growth research**, participating in and leading experiment campaigns flown on:
- **Skylab** (historical dewetting observation referenced/analyzed in his later reviews),
- **TEXUS** sounding-rocket flights (e.g., wetting of InSb melts on crucibles in weightlessness),
- **Spacelab / Spacelab-D1** missions (calorimetric measurement of InSb growth-rate under microgravity),
- **EURECA** (Bridgman growth of GaSb in space, AMF experiments),
- The **Advanced Gradient Heating Facility (AGHF)** of the European Space Agency,
- **International Space Station (ISS)** experiments using the **SUBSA** (Solidification Using a Baffle in Sealed Ampoules) furnace in the NASA Microgravity Science Glovebox, in collaboration with NASA/Marshall Space Flight Center and academic partners (e.g., A. G. Ostrogorsky, Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute), studying Te- and Zn-doped InSb solidification, segregation, and dewetting under pressurized and unpressurized conditions.

This experimental program was carried out in partnership with the **French space agency CNES** and the **European Space Agency (ESA)**, often under CNES–CEA cooperative agreements (e.g., the "GRAMME" agreement), and connected him to an international network of microgravity materials scientists.

### 4.3 Semiconductor and oxide bulk crystal growth

- **InSb, GaSb** — Bridgman/dewetted Bridgman growth, interface dynamics, and segregation, both under microgravity and in ground-based reference experiments (e.g., concentrated InSb–Ga alloys, coupling of thermosolutal convection and interface deformation).
- **CdTe / CdZnTe** — vertical Bridgman growth avoiding crystal–crucible contact via a stable boron-oxide liquid encapsulant layer, relevant to detector-grade crystal quality.
- **Sapphire and Ti-doped sapphire (Ti:Al₂O₃)** — Czochralski growth modelling (interface shape modelling), and — in more recent work — thermodynamic modelling of point-defect chemistry, Ti³⁺/Ti⁴⁺ valence and segregation coefficients as a function of furnace atmosphere and crucible material (Mo crucible, graphite elements), of direct relevance to laser-gain-medium crystal quality.
- **Al₂O₃/YAG eutectic ceramics** — directional solidification, growth-rate effects on colony/microstructure formation, and crystallographic texture/mechanical anisotropy of eutectic composites.
- **Sapphire shaping (GES — "Growth from an Element of Shape")** — real-time control of pulling parameters for growing sapphire hemispherical blanks free of gas and solid-phase inclusions, in collaboration with Russian crystal-growth groups (e.g., V. Kurlov).

### 4.4 Photovoltaic silicon

A significant later-career research direction concerns **directional solidification of multicrystalline silicon for photovoltaics**, including the development of physical models for **twinning and equiaxed grain nucleation/growth** during silicon casting, and the construction of **growth-rate/temperature-gradient ("G–R") charts** as a practical defect-engineering tool to identify process windows that balance crystal quality against production throughput — work that found industrial application in the PV ingot-casting sector.

### 4.5 Defect engineering and process modelling

Across compound-semiconductor and oxide systems alike, Duffar has promoted a unifying **"defect engineering"** approach: using coupled analytical/numerical models of growth rate, thermal gradient, interface shape and capillary boundary conditions to define process windows that minimize structural defects (dislocations, twins, inclusions, grain-boundary formation) while maintaining economically viable growth/production rates.

## 6. Book: Editor of *Crystal Growth Processes Based on Capillarity*

Duffar edited and substantially authored:

> **Duffar, T. (Ed.), *Crystal Growth Processes Based on Capillarity: Czochralski, Floating Zone, Shaping and Crucible Techniques*, John Wiley & Sons, Chichester, 2010.**

This is regarded as a standard reference work bringing together theory and applied/industrial practice on how capillary phenomena (surface tension, wetting, growth angles) govern shape control and defect formation across essentially all major melt-growth techniques. Duffar contributed as editor and as author/co-author of several chapters (including on the vertical Bridgman technique and dewetting), and assembled contributions from leading specialists in the field (including SIMaP colleagues such as Nicolas Eustathopoulos and Béatrice Drevet on wettability fundamentals). At the time of the book's publication, publisher biographical material described him as bringing "over twenty-five years of experience" in crystal growth to the volume, and noted his authorship/co-authorship of more than 100 refereed journal publications.
