## 1. Overview

Jānis Virbulis is a Latvian physicist and numerical modelling specialist, currently **Head of the Laboratory of Semiconductor Technologies** at the **Institute of Numerical Modelling, University of Latvia (LU)**, and a senior researcher and member of the Institute's Scientific Council. He is one of the principal figures in the numerical modelling of industrial silicon crystal growth — both the **Czochralski (CZ)** and **Floating Zone (FZ)** methods — with a career that bridges academic mathematical physics and long-term industrial research at **Wacker Siltronic AG** (Burghausen, Germany), one of the world's leading silicon wafer manufacturers.

His work sits at the intersection of continuum mechanics, magnetohydrodynamics (MHD), heat and mass transfer, and finite-element/finite-volume numerical methods, applied to melt convection, phase-boundary shape control, thermal stress, dopant/impurity segregation, and electromagnetic (magnetic-field-assisted) control of crystal growth processes.

His roughly six-year tenure at Wacker Siltronic (1996–2002), rising to head of its crystal-growth modelling group, gave him direct exposure to the industrial constraints of large-diameter silicon boule production, and forms the technical backbone of most of his subsequent academic output at the University of Latvia — much of which continued in close collaboration with Wacker researchers (notably Wilfried von Ammon and colleagues) for years after his formal departure.

Within the University of Latvia's numerical-modelling tradition, Virbulis worked closely alongside **Andris Muižnieks** (d. 2013), with whom he co-authored the standard review chapter on floating-zone silicon growth. Following Muižnieks's death, Virbulis became the principal continuator of the University of Latvia's semiconductor-crystal-growth modelling programme, now housed within the Institute of Numerical Modelling (successor to the Laboratory for Mathematical Modelling of Environmental and Technological Processes, VTPMML).

## 6. Scientific Contributions

### 6.1 Czochralski silicon growth
Virbulis has been a long-standing contributor to CZ melt-flow modelling, including the influence of steady and dynamic (e.g. cusp, traveling) magnetic fields on silicon melt convection in large-diameter crystal pulling, and to coupled global heat-transfer models predicting the melt–crystal interface shape — directly informing Wacker Siltronic's industrial CZ furnace design (e.g. the Leybold EKZ furnace).

### 6.2 Floating Zone (FZ) silicon growth
A large share of his output addresses the FZ ("needle-eye") technique for large-diameter industrial silicon crystals: modelling of the external triple point, phase-boundary (melting/growth interface) shapes, enthalpy-method treatments of the open melting front on polycrystalline feed rods, coupling with low-frequency induction electromagnetic fields, and reduction of metallic (notably aluminium) contamination during electron-beam melting of feed rods.

### 6.3 Thermal and mechanical stress
He has contributed three-dimensional finite-element modelling of anisotropic thermal/mechanical stress in ⟨100⟩- and ⟨111⟩-oriented silicon crystals grown by both CZ and FZ, examining the influence of process parameters, crystal orientation, and surface ridge formation on stress distribution relevant to dislocation generation and fracture risk.

### 6.4 Reference literature
Together with Andris Muižnieks, Aiga Lüdge, Helge Riemann and Nico Werner, he co-authored the **"Floating Zone Growth of Silicon"** chapter of the *Handbook of Crystal Growth* (2nd edition, Elsevier, 2015) — a standard reference work in the field.

### 6.5 Beyond crystal growth
More recently, his laboratory's numerical-modelling expertise has been applied to problems of indoor airflow, UV-C air-purification device design, and prediction/reduction of airborne infection risk in enclosed spaces — an extension of the group's core CFD/heat-and-mass-transfer competence to public-health-relevant engineering problems.

He also appears as a co-author on unrelated University of Latvia hydrogeological modelling work (the Baltic Artesian Basin groundwater model), reflecting the Institute's broader collaborative modelling activity rather than his personal specialisation.

## 7. Institutional Role

Virbulis currently heads the **Laboratory of Semiconductor Technologies** within the **Institute of Numerical Modelling, University of Latvia** (Jelgavas iela 3, Riga), and sits on the Institute's Scientific Council. The Institute traces its lineage to the University of Latvia's long-standing collaboration with the Institute of Electrotechnology at Leibniz University Hannover, out of which the semiconductor-technologies research direction — originally led jointly by Andris Muižnieks and Jānis Virbulis — developed as one of the Institute's core branches, alongside metallurgical/electrotechnological modelling (led by Andris Jakovičs) and environmental-process modelling.
