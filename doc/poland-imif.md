# The Łukasiewicz Institute of Microelectronics and Photonics (IMiF) as a Partner for Compound-Semiconductor Bulk Crystal Growth and Wafer Manufacturing in Poland

*A technical and economic assessment*

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Łukasiewicz – the Institute of Microelectronics and Photonics (Łukasiewicz–IMiF) is the most credible existing institutional anchor in Poland for a domestic compound-semiconductor bulk crystal growth and wafer manufacturing ecosystem. This is not because it is a green-field actor with ambition alone, but because it inherits, largely intact, the equipment base, process know-how, and personnel of the **Institute of Electronic Materials Technology (ITME)** — the institution that, together with the neighbouring Institute of High Pressure Physics of the Polish Academy of Sciences (UNIPRESS), built Poland's post-war capability in III-V and wide-bandgap semiconductor crystal growth. IMiF was formed in October 2020 from the merger of the **Institute of Electron Technology (ITE)** and **ITME**, and has operated since April 2019 inside the Łukasiewicz Research Network, the applied-research umbrella that replaced Poland's fragmented state-institute system.

The Institute's relevant assets, established through primary-source review, include:

- **Physical vapour transport (PVT) furnaces for bulk SiC crystal growth** (4H/6H polytypes, up to 4-inch diameter, operating to 2500 °C) at the former ITME site on Wólczyńska Street, Warsaw — now also hosting the **Ensemble3 International Research Agenda / Centre of Excellence**, which operates an "A3B5 Compound Semiconductors Group" and an "Oxide Single Crystals Group" as spin-outs of the same campus.
- **More than 30 years of documented III-V monocrystallisation experience** — synthesis, bulk crystal growth, mechanical processing, and characterisation of GaAs, InAs, GaP, InP, GaSb, and InSb — stated explicitly by the Ensemble3 A3B5 group as building on "Łukasiewicz-IMiF's more than 30 years of experience."
- A **full GaN-based device fabrication line** (front-end processing, photolithography, e-beam lithography, thin-film deposition) at the IMiF Warsaw campus, used for power/RF device prototyping including space applications (ESA BIC Poland partner).
- A **SiC PVT-to-device pipeline** (bulk growth → homoepitaxy → device processing) documented in the Institute's own technical literature, including detailed correlation studies between substrate quality and 4H-N SiC homoepitaxial layers.
- Organisational structure around four "technology lines": optoelectronic subassemblies, silicon subassemblies, wide-bandgap semiconductor subassemblies, and LTCC (low-temperature co-fired ceramics) technology — i.e., bulk-to-device vertical integration is already the Institute's stated operating model, not an aspiration.
- Historical adjacency to the **Jan Czochralski legacy** (the Czochralski method was developed at Warsaw University of Technology, and Ensemble3's own materials describe itself as building "on the pioneering work of Prof. Czochralski").

Set against this, IMiF's crystal-growth capability is **sub-industrial in scale** relative to the wafer diameters (150–200 mm SiC, 150 mm GaN) and volumes now standard among global suppliers (Wolfspeed, Coherent/II-VI, SICC, SK Siltron, Sumitomo, Freiberger). The PVT tool base tops out at 4-inch (100 mm) SiC boules — one to two full wafer generations behind the current commercial state of the art — and there is no public evidence of a qualified, high-volume ingot-to-wafer production line (slicing, lapping, polishing, epi-ready specification, defect metrology at scale) inside IMiF comparable to a merchant substrate supplier. IMiF is therefore best understood, and best used, as a **pilot-line and technology-transfer partner**, not as a ready-made substitute for a merchant fab. This matches the structural logic of the EU Chips Act 2.0 and the nine-country Semiconductor Coalition (which includes Poland), whose own architecture is built around pilot lines rather than immediate full-scale fabs.

## 3. Technical Capabilities Relevant to Bulk Crystal Growth and Wafer Technology

### 3.1 Silicon carbide (SiC): physical vapour transport (PVT / modified Lely method)

The clearest, most directly documented bulk-crystal-growth capability is SiC PVT growth at the Wólczyńska Street (DFM-ITME) site:

- **Three PVT furnaces**, each with **two independent resistance heaters** enabling active control of the axial/radial temperature field during growth — a key handle for polytype stability (4H vs. 6H selection) and for suppressing micropipe and basal-plane-dislocation formation.
- **Maximum operating temperature 2500 °C**, consistent with the sublimation growth regime required for SiC (typical PVT growth temperatures are 2000–2400 °C).
- **Crystal diameter capability up to 4 inches (100 mm)**.
- Process gas supply for **argon and nitrogen**, the latter relevant for n-type doping via nitrogen incorporation during growth and for growth-atmosphere partial-pressure control, which governs Si/C stoichiometry management in the vapour phase.
- A downstream **homoepitaxy programme**: IMiF's technical literature includes a documented correlation study between 4H-N SiC substrate properties (resistivity uniformity, surface topography, roughness, crystalline quality) and CVD-homoepitaxial layer quality, using mercury-probe C–V, FTIR reflectance, AFM, flatness metrology, and X-ray diffraction — i.e., the Institute operates the full substrate-to-epiwafer characterisation loop, not just crystal pulling in isolation.

**Assessment against the state of the art.** Commercial SiC substrate suppliers (Wolfspeed, Coherent, SK Siltron, SICC, TankeBlue) are now qualifying 200 mm (8-inch) SiC and shipping 150 mm (6-inch) as the mainstream commercial diameter. IMiF's 100 mm ceiling places it roughly one full wafer generation behind the industrial state of the art — a materially important gap, because SiC PVT scale-up is not a simple linear extrapolation: larger diameters amplify radial thermal gradients, exacerbate micropipe and basal-plane-dislocation densities, and require substantially re-engineered crucible/insulation thermal-field design (see §5). This is the central technical challenge any partnership must confront explicitly rather than assume away.

### 3.2 III-V compound semiconductors (GaAs, InP, GaSb, InSb, InAs, GaP)

The strongest single evidentiary claim available is from Ensemble3's own institutional description of its A3B5 (III-V) Compound Semiconductors Group, which states its expertise is founded on **"Łukasiewicz-IMiF's more than 30 years of experience in the technology of AIIIBV compounds, including synthesis, monocrystallization, mechanical processing and characterization."** Concretely, the group states it undertakes:

- R&D of manufacturing processes for single crystals and alloys;
- structural, electrical, and optical characterisation and purity assessment of semiconductor materials;
- **production of GaAs, InAs, GaP, InP, GaSb, InSb**, and specialty high-purity alloys "on a small scale";
- development of fabrication methods for **thermoelectric materials, topological insulators, and spintronic materials**.

This maps directly onto the historical ITME core competency: III-V bulk growth by liquid-encapsulated Czochralski (LEC) and Bridgman/vertical-gradient-freeze variants, the two dominant industrial methods for GaAs and InP boule growth (LEC historically dominant for semi-insulating GaAs; VGF/VB increasingly favoured industrially for improved thermal-stress control and lower dislocation density). The explicit qualifier "on a small scale" is the operative constraint: this reads as a materials-science production capability (feedstock synthesis, small-boule growth, wafer-level characterisation for R&D and specialty/niche applications), not a qualified commercial-volume substrate line.

### 3.3 Gallium nitride (GaN): device-level, not bulk-crystal-level, strength

A structurally important distinction for this report: IMiF's *documented* GaN capability is overwhelmingly at the **epitaxy-and-device** level, not bulk boule growth. Per the ESA Business Incubation Centre Poland's IMiF profile, the Institute:

> "has a full production line for manufacturing nitride-based semiconductor instruments, performs photolithography and electronolithography processes, thin film deposition processes for metals, semiconductors and dielectrics by cathode sputtering, electron beam sputtering..."

and offers prototyping support for GaN-based devices for space applications (microwave power electronics, power electronics systems) spanning modelling/simulation, device topology design, fabrication-process development, and full electrical/thermal/frequency characterisation.

**This is distinct from bulk single-crystal GaN growth** (ammonothermal, HVPE, or Na-flux methods), which in Poland is historically centred at the **Institute of High Pressure Physics, Polish Academy of Sciences (UNIPRESS)** — the institution behind the Porowski high-nitrogen-pressure solution growth method and the ammonothermal patents (Dwiliński et al.) that underpin the Ammono / spin-out lineage of Polish native-substrate GaN. IMiF's own public materials do not evidence in-house bulk GaN boule growth; its GaN strength is upstream-material-agnostic device fabrication, most plausibly consuming externally sourced (including UNIPRESS/Ammono, or foreign HVPE) GaN or GaN-on-substrate epiwafers, or growing GaN heteroepitaxially (MOCVD, likely on sapphire, SiC, or Si) rather than as free-standing native bulk boules. **A partnership pursuing native bulk GaN substrates should treat IMiF and UNIPRESS/Ammono as complementary, not overlapping, capabilities**, and should explicitly verify current-state bulk-GaN activity with IMiF directly, since institutional boundaries in this space have shifted with the Łukasiewicz consolidation.

### 3.4 Oxide single crystals and specialty substrates

The co-located Ensemble3 Centre of Excellence operates a dedicated "Oxide Single Crystals Group" and "Oxide Single Crystals Laboratory" on the same Wólczyńska campus, continuing ITME's historical strength in Czochralski-grown oxide crystals (laser hosts, scintillators, substrate oxides such as sapphire, and related functional-oxide single crystals). While outside the strict "compound semiconductor" scope, this is directly relevant as (a) a shared thermal/mechanical/metrology infrastructure base, and (b) a potential substrate-technology adjacency (e.g., epitaxial templates, lattice-matched buffer layers) for compound-semiconductor heteroepitaxy.

### 3.5 Wide-bandgap device processing and the "technology line" model

IMiF organises its R&D explicitly around four **technology lines**: optoelectronic subassemblies, silicon subassemblies, **wide-bandgap semiconductor subassemblies**, and LTCC. The Institute's own materials list a project portfolio spanning "hydrogen technologies, flake graphene, epitaxial graphene, high-frequency gallium nitride-based power devices, silicon carbide-based power devices, crystals, lasers, white light sources, chromium masks, and diffractive elements." The stated intent — to run these as end-to-end lines open to both scientific participants and industrial partners — is structurally the correct model for a bulk-to-wafer-to-device ecosystem; the open question, addressed in §5, is one of scale, yield, and qualification maturity, not conceptual design.

## 4. IMiF Within the Broader Polish and European Ecosystem

### 4.1 The domestic constellation

A realistic assessment of "Poland's compound-semiconductor ecosystem" cannot treat IMiF in isolation. The relevant actors are:

- **Łukasiewicz–IMiF** (Warsaw/Kraków) — bulk SiC and III-V crystal growth, GaN device fabrication, LTCC, wide-bandgap subassemblies.
- **Institute of High Pressure Physics, PAS (UNIPRESS)** — high-nitrogen-pressure and ammonothermal bulk GaN growth (native GaN substrates); commercialised historically via Ammono.
- **Ensemble3** (International Research Agenda / EU-funded Centre of Excellence, Warsaw, Wólczyńska campus) — co-located with IMiF's former ITME site; operates as a semi-independent legal entity (Ensemble3 sp. z o.o.) but shares physical infrastructure, historical staff lineage, and research scope (A3B5 compounds, oxide single crystals, functional materials) with IMiF. This co-location is a material asset: it effectively doubles the addressable expertise and equipment base available on a single Warsaw campus, provided formal collaboration agreements are structured to bridge the two organisations.
- **Łukasiewicz–PORT** (Wrocław) — MOVPE epitaxy of III-nitride heterostructures (Advanced Semiconductor Epitaxy Research Group, led by Prof. Detlef Hommel), industrial collaborations (e.g., with ams OSRAM) on boron-doped III-nitride heterostructures — a complementary epitaxy node to IMiF's device-processing strength.
- **VIGO Photonics** (Ożarów Mazowiecki) — the leading Polish commercial IR-detector and photonics company, publicly active in shaping national semiconductor-sector policy proposals (training, startup incubation, cluster promotion).
- **Polish Investment and Trade Agency (PAIH)** — publishes an annual "Semiconductors Industry in Poland" report mapping the national manufacturing ecosystem, workforce, and investment-support instruments, and explicitly frames Poland's ecosystem as spanning science-to-business linkages.

### 4.2 European policy and funding architecture

Poland is a **founding member of the nine-country EU Semiconductor Coalition** (launched March 2025, alongside Austria, Belgium, Finland, France, Germany, Italy, Spain, and the Netherlands), committing to reinforced cooperation on research, production-capacity expansion, and workforce development under the European Chips Act umbrella. The European Chips Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/1781) has to date catalysed roughly **€69 billion** across R&D and facility investments (as of October 2025), split roughly between the IPCEI on Microelectronics and Communication Technologies (€21.8bn), Chips Joint Undertaking R&D pilot lines (€5.1bn), and confirmed/pending First-of-a-Kind and non-FOAK facility investments (€53.8bn). A **Chips Act 2.0** was proposed by the European Commission in June 2026 as part of a broader Tech Sovereignty Package, explicitly responding to acknowledged shortcomings of the first Act (most visibly, Intel's July 2025 cancellation of its planned Magdeburg fab) and to surging AI-driven demand.

**Strategic implication for IMiF.** The Chips Act's operative funding architecture is pilot-line-centric, not mega-fab-centric, for exactly the tier of institution IMiF represents. IMiF is realistically positioned to compete for Chips JU pilot-line funding, IPCEI participation as a materials/substrate-technology node, and Horizon Europe / Digital Europe consortium roles (the Institute already participates in EU brokerage events such as Horizon4Photonics), rather than to anchor a First-of-a-Kind commercial fab on its own balance sheet. This is a *feature*, not a limitation, for the specific ecosystem-building question posed in this report: Poland's near-term opportunity is very plausibly a qualified pilot/specialty-substrate line (SiC and/or III-V) feeding downstream European device fabs, rather than a head-to-head volume play against Wolfspeed- or SICC-scale suppliers.

### 4.3 Market and competitive context

Global commercial SiC and GaN substrate supply is presently concentrated among a small number of vertically integrated players (Wolfspeed, Coherent/II-VI, SK Siltron, SICC, TankeBlue, Sumitomo Electric, Freiberger Compound Materials for III-V), most of which have already qualified or are qualifying 150–200 mm SiC and are investing heavily in defect-density reduction at scale. The Poland-specific semiconductor market itself remains modest (Poland's overall semiconductor market is projected at roughly USD 2.8–3.5 billion, 2025–2030, dominated by established multinational manufacturing/assembly presence — Intel Technology Poland, Infineon Poland, STMicroelectronics Poland, NXP Poland, Texas Instruments Poland — rather than substrate/materials production). This confirms that a Polish bulk-crystal ecosystem, if built around IMiF, would necessarily be an **export- and EU-supply-chain-oriented specialty play**, not a play against domestic device demand alone, which is currently too small on its own to underwrite substrate-fab-scale capital investment.

## 5. Critical Technical Gaps and Risks

An economically informed assessment must state the gaps plainly rather than assume they will resolve themselves through funding alone.

1. **Diameter and yield gap.** IMiF's documented SiC PVT ceiling (4-inch/100 mm) trails the commercial mainstream (150 mm, moving to 200 mm) by a full node. Scaling PVT SiC growth to 150–200 mm is not a matter of building a bigger furnace; it requires re-solved thermal-field design (the temperature gradients that drive sublimation transport and that must simultaneously suppress micropipes, basal-plane dislocations, and polytype inclusions scale non-trivially with crucible diameter), longer growth-run engineering, and substantially higher seed-crystal and process-control sophistication. This is the single largest capital- and know-how-intensive gap.
2. **Absence of documented high-volume wafering infrastructure.** Bulk growth is only the first of several value-adding steps to a qualified epi-ready wafer: ingot X-ray orientation, wire-saw slicing, edge-grinding, lapping, chemical-mechanical polishing (CMP), and surface/sub-surface defect metrology at production tolerances (sub-nanometre roughness, tight total-thickness-variation and warp/bow specs). None of the sources reviewed document a qualified, throughput-scale wafering line at IMiF comparable to merchant substrate suppliers; this would likely need to be built or partnered (e.g., with an existing European wafering specialist) rather than assumed present.
3. **Institutional fragmentation of native bulk GaN.** Native bulk GaN substrate technology in Poland sits primarily with UNIPRESS/Ammono, not IMiF. A Polish "compound semiconductor ecosystem" strategy that wants native GaN substrates (as opposed to heteroepitaxial GaN-on-SiC/GaN-on-Si) must explicitly bring UNIPRESS into scope; treating IMiF alone as sufficient for GaN would be a material strategic error.
4. **Small-scale framing of III-V production.** Ensemble3's own description of GaAs/InP/GaSb/InSb/InAs/GaP production explicitly qualifies it as "small scale." This should be read as a genuine, credible R&D and specialty-materials capability — appropriate for detector materials, thermoelectrics, topological-insulator and spintronic research substrates, and low-volume/high-value niches — but not as evidence of qualified commercial-volume wafer supply.
5. **Talent and capital intensity of scale-up.** Bulk crystal growth is a notoriously slow-iterating, capital- and expertise-intensive discipline (growth runs measured in days to weeks per boule; defect-reduction learning curves measured in years). Poland's broader semiconductor workforce base, while present (Intel, Infineon, ST, NXP, TI all operate design/assembly/test facilities in Poland), is not primarily oriented around bulk-crystal-growth expertise; VIGO Photonics and others have publicly flagged training and workforce development as a national policy gap requiring dedicated investment.
6. **Legal/organisational seam between IMiF and Ensemble3.** Although co-located and thematically overlapping, Ensemble3 (Ensemble3 sp. z o.o.) is formally a distinct legal entity (an EU-funded International Research Agenda / Centre of Excellence hosted by, but not identical to, IMiF). Any partnership agreement must clarify IP ownership, equipment-access rights, and personnel dual-affiliation explicitly, since institutional overlap can create ambiguity in contracting rather than resolve it automatically.

### 6.1 Recommended partnership posture

For an organisation or public body seeking to establish an advanced compound-semiconductor crystal-growth and wafer ecosystem in Poland, the evidence supports the following posture toward IMiF:

- **Treat IMiF as the pilot-line and technology-transfer anchor**, not as an already-qualified merchant substrate supplier. Its realistic near-term role is de-risking process technology (SiC PVT scale-up beyond 100 mm; III-V growth-process refinement) and feeding specialty/niche demand (space, RF/power prototyping, detector materials, research substrates) while a scaled wafering/qualification capability is built out, likely through a dedicated capital programme (plausibly EU Chips JU pilot-line co-funding) rather than organic institute budget.
- **Bundle IMiF with UNIPRESS/Ammono explicitly** for any strategy requiring native bulk GaN, rather than assuming IMiF alone covers the full nitride value chain.
- **Leverage the Ensemble3 co-location** as a genuine asset (shared Wólczyńska campus, complementary A3B5 and oxide-crystal groups) but formalise the IMiF/Ensemble3 relationship contractually before committing capital or IP.
- **Frame the investment case around EU supply-chain indispensability**, consistent with the direction signalled by Chips Act 2.0 and the Semiconductor Coalition, rather than around Polish domestic device demand, which is currently too small to justify substrate-fab-scale capacity on its own.
- **Prioritise diameter scale-up (SiC toward 150 mm) and a qualified wafering line as the two highest-leverage capital investments**, since these are the two most clearly documented gaps between current IMiF capability and commercial substrate-supplier parity.
