# Professor Dorota A. Pawlak: Contributions to Semiconductor Crystal Growth, Bulk Crystal Technology, and Advanced Photonic Materials

*A technical and scientific briefing for researchers, engineers, technology investors, and policymakers*

---

## Executive Summary

Professor Dorota A. Pawlak is one of Europe's most prominent figures at the intersection of bulk crystal growth, compound semiconductor technology, and nanophotonics. Based in Warsaw, Poland, she holds appointments as a professor affiliated with the University of Warsaw's Centre of New Technologies and the Łukasiewicz Research Network's Institute of Microelectronics and Photonics (formerly the Institute of Electronic Materials Technology, ITME), and she is the founding President of **ENSEMBLE3**, a Teaming-for-Excellence/International Research Agenda "Centre of Excellence for Nanophotonics, Advanced Materials and Novel Crystal Growth-Based Technologies" that she built into an internationally networked institute of roughly 100 researchers and staff. Her scientific signature is the use of **directional solidification and self-organization phenomena in eutectic and bulk oxide systems** to manufacture functional electromagnetic and photonic materials — metamaterials, plasmonic nanocomposites, and photonic components — through bottom-up crystal growth rather than conventional top-down lithographic fabrication. Alongside this fundamental materials science, she leads one of Europe's few integrated laboratories producing **III-V compound semiconductor single crystals (GaAs, GaSb, GaP, InP, InAs, InSb)** by Czochralski and related melt-growth methods, positioning her work squarely within the industrial semiconductor materials supply chain relevant to optoelectronics, RF/microwave electronics, infrared imaging, and, increasingly, photonic integration and defense-relevant substrate technologies.

For an audience assessing semiconductor manufacturing capability and crystal-growth technology in Europe, Pawlak's career is significant for three converging reasons: (1) she has sustained and modernized Poland's long-standing (Czochralski-linked) tradition in compound semiconductor bulk crystal growth at a moment when Europe is seeking to reduce dependency on non-European substrate and materials supply; (2) she pioneered a widely cited alternative route to metamaterials and plasmonic structures via self-organized eutectic solidification, an approach with potential manufacturing-cost advantages over top-down nanofabrication; and (3) she has translated this scientific program into large-scale European funding instruments (H2020 Teaming, Foundation for Polish Science International Research Agendas) and institution-building, culminating in ENSEMBLE3 — giving her outsized influence on European advanced-materials policy, including a role in the European Commission's Technology Council on Advanced Materials.

## 1. Background and Career Trajectory

- **Education:** M.Sc. (1994, crystallochemistry), Ph.D. (1999, crystallochemistry), and D.Sc./habilitation (2011–2013, physicochemistry of solid state), all in chemistry from the University of Warsaw.
- **Early institutional base:** From 1994, she was a staff member of the **Institute of Electronic Materials Technology (ITME)** in Warsaw — historically Poland's largest technological research institute focused on electronic and optoelectronic materials and components, and an institution with direct lineage to Jan Czochralski, the Polish chemist who invented the Czochralski crystal-pulling method underlying the majority of the world's silicon and compound-semiconductor single-crystal production.
- **International research experience:** She spent two years (2000–2001) as a research associate at the **Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University**, Sendai, Japan — a leading global center for crystal growth science — which shaped her subsequent work on melt-growth techniques including micro-pulling-down (μ-PD) crystal growth.
- **Institutional leadership:** She became Head of the Department of Functional Materials at ITME (later restructured into the Łukasiewicz Research Network's Institute of Microelectronics and Photonics, Ł-IMiF), and established a parallel Laboratory of Materials Technologies at the Centre of New Technologies, University of Warsaw, giving her a dual academic–applied research base.
- **Professional service:** President of the Polish Society for Crystal Growth (2013–2015); Director on the Board of Directors of the Virtual Institute of Metamorphose (metamaterials) since 2007; and, more recently, a shortlisted candidate for the Board of Stakeholders of **Photonics21**, the European public–private photonics technology platform with over 4,000 members.
- **Recognition:** Foundation for Polish Science (FNP) prize for young scientists (2000); laureate of the "Woman-Innovator 2011" competition in Poland; two FNP TEAM project grants; and research funding from international agencies including the US **Air Force Office of Scientific Research (AFOSR)**, an indicator of the dual-use/defense-adjacent relevance of her materials work.

## 2. Scientific Contributions

### 2.1 Self-organized eutectic solidification as a route to metamaterials and plasmonics

Pawlak's most distinctive scientific contribution is the proposal and experimental development of **directionally solidified eutectic composites as a "bottom-up" manufacturing platform for metamaterials and plasmonic materials** — an idea she introduced as novel within the international crystal-growth and photonics communities. Conventional metamaterials (engineered composites exhibiting properties such as negative refractive index) are built top-down using lithography and nanofabrication, which is slow and expensive to scale to large volumes. Pawlak's group instead exploits the natural self-organization of eutectic melts during controlled solidification (including via the micro-pulling-down method) to grow, in a single processing step, ordered micro- and nanoscale two-phase or multi-phase microstructures — rods, lamellae, and fractal-like split-tube geometries — that mimic the building blocks of engineered electromagnetic metamaterials.

Representative results include:

- Demonstration that directional solidification of eutectics can produce **self-organized particles with split-ring-resonator-like cross-sections** — the canonical unit cell of negative-refractive-index metamaterials — with a fractal microstructure and high anisotropy, achievable in bulk-scale pieces rather than small lithographic patches.
- Development of the **NanoParticle Direct Doping (NPDD)** method, in which nanoparticles are directly incorporated into a directionally solidified dielectric matrix to manufacture three-dimensional bulk plasmonic nanocomposites — offering a scalable alternative to layer-by-layer nanoplasmonic fabrication.
- Studies of **self-organized rod-like eutectic microstructures** (e.g., Tb₃Sc₂Al₃O₁₂–TbScO₃:Pr, ZnO–ZnWO₄, SrTiO₃–TiO₂, NiTiO₃–TiO₂, Bi₂Te₃–Te) exploring how eutectic phase geometry and composition can be tuned to produce tailored optical filtering, polarization-selective, photoelectrochemical, thermoelectric, and photocarrier-transport behavior.
- Work on **tunable narrowband optical filters and polarizers** based on ZnO–ZnWO₄ eutectic composites, and on **terahertz emission and photocarrier dynamics** in Bi₂Te₃–Te eutectic p–n heterostructures, connecting her crystal-growth platform to THz optoelectronics and thermoelectric device physics.
- Broader reviews and contributions on **whispering-gallery-mode microresonators** and **supramolecular chirality synchronization in plasmonic nanocomposite films**, indicating a research program that spans from bulk crystal growth through nanoscale self-assembly to device-level photonic characterization.

The scientific significance of this body of work is that it reframes eutectic solidification — traditionally studied mainly for structural alloys — as a **materials-by-design toolkit for photonics and electromagnetics**, with the crystal grower's control parameters (growth rate, thermal gradient, composition, dopant partitioning) substituting for a lithographer's mask design. This is conceptually important for cost-effective scaling of metamaterial and plasmonic components beyond niche laboratory samples.

### 2.2 Oxide single-crystal growth (Czochralski and micro-pulling-down methods)

Pawlak's group has extensive published work on **Czochralski growth of rare-earth aluminate and garnet-type oxide single crystals**, including:

- First-ever reported Czochralski growth of **Prₓ La₁₋ₓ AlO₃ solid-solution crystals** across a range of praseodymium concentrations, with characterization of growth morphology (spiral growth at high Pr content), optical (color) changes with composition, thermal conductivity, and magnetic properties, in collaboration with researchers including M. A. Carpenter (University of Cambridge).
- Companion studies using **synchrotron X-ray diffraction topography** to characterize domain structure and crystallographic defects (twin domain systems) in these Czochralski-grown crystals — methodologically important for defect engineering in oxide substrates used in photonic and magneto-optic applications.
- Structural studies of garnet crystals (e.g., Y₃Al₅O₁₂) using solid-state NMR spectroscopy, and broader crystallochemical characterization work reflecting her original training in crystallochemistry.
- Use of the **micro-pulling-down (μ-PD) method** — a rapid, shaped melt-growth technique she encountered during her Tohoku University tenure — for growing fibrous and self-organized eutectic microstructures (e.g., PrAlO₃–PrAl₁₁O₁₈), enabling faster iteration on eutectic composition/microstructure relationships than bulk Czochralski growth allows.

This body of work situates Pawlak within the mainstream tradition of melt-growth oxide crystal science (relevant to laser hosts, scintillators, substrates, and magneto-optic/nonlinear-optic materials), while her eutectic and self-organization work extends that tradition into new application space.

### 2.3 Compound semiconductor (III-V) bulk crystal growth and wafer-relevant technology

Beyond oxide and eutectic photonic materials, Pawlak's institutional base (Łukasiewicz-IMiF, formerly ITME) operates one of Europe's few integrated **III-V compound semiconductor single-crystal growth laboratories**, with more than 30–40 years of accumulated expertise predating her leadership and now organized under ENSEMBLE3 as the **A3B5 (III-V) Compound Semiconductors Group and Laboratory**. This laboratory:

- Grows and processes single crystals and alloys of **GaAs, InAs, GaP, InP, GaSb, and InSb** — materials foundational to high-electron-mobility RF/microwave devices, LEDs and laser diodes, infrared photodetectors, solar cells, and thermoelectric and topological-insulator research.
- Explicitly traces its methodological lineage to **Jan Czochralski**, the founder of the crystal-pulling method that underlies the overwhelming majority of the world's silicon and compound-semiconductor wafer production — a heritage ENSEMBLE3 markets prominently as institutional differentiation in the European advanced-materials landscape.
- Covers the **full materials value chain** relevant to wafer manufacturing: synthesis of source compounds, single-crystal (monocrystallization) growth, mechanical processing (cutting, shaping — the upstream steps of wafer fabrication), and materials characterization (electrical, structural, optical, purity).
- Extends into **topological insulators and spintronic materials**, and thermoelectric module manufacturing technology, indicating diversification beyond legacy III-V compounds into materials relevant to quantum and low-power electronics.

For technology investors and policymakers, this laboratory is one of the more complete European bulk-crystal-to-substrate pipelines for III-V compound semiconductors — materials that, unlike silicon, are not produced at meaningful scale within the EU and are increasingly strategically salient for defense, satellite/space (radiation-hard GaAs solar cells), 5G/6G RF front-ends, and infrared sensing supply chains.

## 3. Institution-Building: ENSEMBLE3 Centre of Excellence

Pawlak's largest-scale contribution may be organizational: she conceived, secured funding for, and now presides over **ENSEMBLE3** ("Centre of ExcelleNce for nanophotonicS, advancEd Materials and novel crystal growth-Based technoLogiEs"), established in Warsaw in 2020 (company registered as Ensemble3 sp. z o.o.) under the EU's **Horizon 2020 Teaming for Excellence** instrument (Grant Agreement No. 763798, ~15 million EUR budget) combined with a **Foundation for Polish Science International Research Agendas Programme (IRAP)** grant (~30 million EUR across her combined project leadership).

Key features:

- **Strategic partners** include the University of Warsaw, Sapienza Università di Roma, the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology, the Łukasiewicz Institute of Microelectronics and Photonics, CIC nanoGUNE (Basque Foundation for Science), and Poland's National Centre for Research and Development — giving ENSEMBLE3 a genuinely pan-European research network rather than a purely national one.
- **Research groups** under Pawlak's institute span Functional Materials Technology, Oxide Single Crystals, A3B5 (III-V) Compound Semiconductors, Optical Nanocharacterization, Inverse Materials Design, Next-Generation Energy Systems, Solar Energy Conversion, and Biophotonic Applications — an unusually broad materials-to-device stack under one roof.
- **Application domains** the centre lists for its crystal-growth-derived materials include photonics, optoelectronics, AI hardware, aerospace, defense, security, high-speed optical communications and data centers, quantum computing, THz systems, infrared imaging, high-performance chips, high-temperature/high-voltage power electronics, lasers, optical isolators, nonlinear optics, substrate materials, and scintillators — reflecting deliberate positioning at the interface of fundamental crystal growth and dual-use/defense-relevant technology areas.
- **Policy engagement:** In November 2024, Pawlak represented Ensemble3 — the only Polish science/innovation-sector organization present — at the inaugural meeting in Brussels of the European Commission's newly established **Technology Council on Advanced Materials** (created July 2024), alongside Polish government representatives from the Ministry of Economic Development and Technology, indicating direct input into EU advanced-materials strategy.
- Under her leadership, ENSEMBLE3 published a 2024 study in ***Nature Communications*** described as combining materials technology, experimental, and theoretical work from a broad collaborative team — evidence of continued high-impact scientific output from the institute she leads.

## 4. Assessment for Different Audiences

**For scientists and engineers:** Pawlak's eutectic self-organization platform is a genuinely distinctive methodological contribution — it offers a scalable, single-step alternative to lithographic nanofabrication for a subset of metamaterial and plasmonic geometries, and her group's parallel oxide and III-V bulk-growth expertise gives her program unusual vertical depth from melt chemistry to device characterization (THz, photoelectrochemical, thermoelectric).

**For technology investors:** ENSEMBLE3 represents a rare, EU-based integrated crystal-growth-to-materials pipeline covering both compound semiconductors (III-V) and functional oxide/eutectic photonic materials, at a time when Europe's semiconductor and photonics strategies are actively seeking domestic upstream materials capability. The institute's explicit listing of defense, aerospace, quantum, and data-center applications signals alignment with high-growth, strategically funded sectors.

**For policymakers:** Pawlak's direct participation in the European Commission's Technology Council on Advanced Materials, and her institute's role as the sole Polish representative in that body, make her a relevant reference point for EU advanced-materials and critical-raw-materials/semiconductor-sovereignty policy discussions, particularly regarding compound-semiconductor substrate capacity within the EU.
