## 4. Field of Research

Vizman's scientific work sits at the intersection of **transport phenomena, magnetohydrodynamics (MHD), and numerical simulation applied to melt crystal growth**, spanning both semiconductor and functional-crystal (fluoride, oxide) systems. His declared areas of specialization are:

- Crystal growth processes
- Transport phenomena (heat, mass, and momentum transfer in melts)
- Numerical modelling (finite volume, finite element, and lattice-Boltzmann methods; parallel computation)
- Magnetohydrodynamics

### 4.1 Principal research threads

1. **Semi-transparent and shaped-crystal growth (early career, 1990s).** Heat transfer analysis, thermal stress, and interface-shape prediction in EFG- and Bridgman-grown fluoride and sapphire crystals, in collaboration with I. Nicoara and D. Nicoara at Timișoara.

2. **Magnetic-field-controlled Czochralski (Cz) growth of silicon and GaAs (Erlangen collaboration, late 1990s–2000s).** Development and application of fully 3D, time-dependent numerical models of melt convection under rotating (RMF), travelling (TMF), cusp, vertical, and horizontal magnetic fields, validated directly against experimental temperature and interface-shape measurements obtained at the Fraunhofer IISB and at industrial partners (including NEC, Japan, for the Electromagnetic Czochralski/EMCZ process). This body of work established him as the lead developer of the **STHAMAS3D** simulation code, used for optimizing industrial-scale Si and GaAs Czochralski growth.

3. **Directional solidification and multicrystalline silicon for photovoltaics (2010s).** 3D coupled simulations of melt flow, dopant/oxygen segregation, interface shape, and crucible dissolution in directional-solidification (DS) casting of multicrystalline silicon ingots, including the effect of travelling magnetic fields and mechanical/electromagnetic stirring — much of it in collaboration with J. Friedrich (Fraunhofer IISB) and K. Dadzis (later at the Leibniz Institute for Crystal Growth, IKZ Berlin).

4. **Model-experiment validation and open, benchmarkable Czochralski models (2020s).** Continued collaboration with the IKZ Berlin group (K. Dadzis and coworkers) on developing and validating 2D/3D thermal and multiphysical Czochralski furnace models against dedicated model experiments (e.g., tin, CsI), addressing the field-wide problem that many published Cz models are not independently reproducible.

5. **Growth and spectroscopic characterization of doped alkaline-earth fluoride crystals (2020s, current focus).** Bridgman–Stockbarger growth, coupled with numerical simulation of the growth process and segregation, of CaF₂- and BaF₂-based crystals doped with rare-earth and lanthanide ions (Er³⁺, Tm²⁺/Tm³⁺, Tb³⁺, Ce³⁺), for magneto-optical and scintillation/luminescence applications, carried out with a Timișoara–Grenoble (Institut Néel) collaboration involving P. Veber, G. Buse, C. Schornig, M. Stef, and others.

6. **Laser–plasma and high-energy-density physics (recent, interdisciplinary).** Co-authored work on laser-driven electron acceleration from near-critical-density targets for γ-photon generation, in collaboration with E. d'Humières (Bordeaux) — reflecting a broadening of his numerical/computational-physics activity beyond crystal growth.

## 9. Selected Software Contribution

Vizman is the **lead developer of the STHAMAS3D program**, a three-dimensional numerical simulation code developed principally during his Humboldt fellowship period at the Fraunhofer Institute IISB, Erlangen, for modelling and optimizing coupled heat transfer, melt convection, magnetohydrodynamics, and interface-shape evolution in industrial melt-growth processes (Czochralski, Vertical Gradient Freeze, directional solidification). The code has been used over the years by leading world producers of silicon and GaAs crystals for process optimization.

## 11. Main Scientific Contributions (as summarized in his own CV)

- Growth and characterization of a range of crystals (fluoride and silicon systems).
- Design and construction of a model experiment to study the combined influence of a static magnetic field and an applied electrical current on melt flow in semiconducting melts.
- 3D modelling and optimization of the Vertical Gradient Freeze (VGF) process, including use of the Rosseland approximation for radiative heat transfer in semi-transparent crystals.
- 3D modelling and optimization of the Si-Czochralski process, including prediction of melt temperature fluctuations for varying crystal/crucible rotation rates, validated against experiment.
- Prediction of the influence of vertical, horizontal, and cusp magnetic fields on solid–liquid interface shape and melt flow across Czochralski, Bridgman, and VGF growth processes.
- Modelling of oxygen transport in Czochralski-grown silicon single crystals.
- Modelling of the Electromagnetic Czochralski (EMCZ) process, validated against interface-shape data measured at NEC, Japan.
- Study of the influence of melt flow on interface shape during silicon ingot casting.
- Modelling of melt flow and interface shape in industrial liquid-encapsulated Czochralski (LEC) growth of GaAs.

## 13. Summary

Daniel Vizman is a Romanian physicist who has spent his entire academic career at the West University of Timișoara, rising from junior teaching assistant (1992) to full professor (2008) and serving as Director of its Doctoral School in Physics (2012–2016). His scientific identity was forged through two formative international fellowships in Erlangen, Germany (DAAD, 1995–96; Humboldt, 1999–2000), where he became the lead developer of the STHAMAS3D code and a central figure in the numerical modelling of magnetic-field-controlled Czochralski crystal growth — work later extended to directional solidification of photovoltaic-grade multicrystalline silicon. In the past decade his research has diversified into the Bridgman–Stockbarger growth and spectroscopic characterization of rare-earth-doped alkaline-earth fluoride crystals (in partnership with the Institut Néel, Grenoble) and into laser–plasma physics, while continuing his long-standing validation-focused collaboration with the Leibniz Institute for Crystal Growth (IKZ) in Berlin. His contributions are recognized through an h-index of 12+ (332+ non-self citations as of 2016, with substantially more citations accrued since), a Young Scientist Award from ICCG-13 (2001), an Elsevier reviewing excellence award and the Romanian Academy's "Constantin Miculescu" award (both 2014), and sustained roles on the scientific committees of the field's principal international conferences and workshops.
