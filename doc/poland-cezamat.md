# CEZAMAT and the Prospects for an Advanced Compound-Semiconductor Crystal Growth and Wafer Ecosystem in Poland

## Executive Summary

CEZAMAT (Centre for Advanced Materials and Technologies) at Warsaw University of Technology (WUT) is Poland's most credible institutional anchor for a domestic compound-semiconductor manufacturing ecosystem, but it is **not itself a bulk crystal growth facility**. Its comparative advantage lies in wafer-scale front-end processing, device integration, and system demonstration — most concretely evidenced by its selection as the sole Polish partner in the EU Chips Act's FAMES pilot line (FD-SOI, €830M). Poland's actual bulk crystal growth competence — high-nitrogen-pressure and ammonothermal GaN — resides at the **Institute of High Pressure Physics, Polish Academy of Sciences (IHPP PAS / "Unipress")**, which is a CEZAMAT consortium member institute, not part of CEZAMAT's own laboratories. This distinction matters: any strategy for building a Polish compound-semiconductor crystal-growth-to-wafer value chain must treat CEZAMAT and Unipress as complementary nodes — Unipress supplying bulk GaN crystals and native substrates, CEZAMAT supplying cleanroom processing, epitaxy, device fabrication, characterization, and the 200 mm-class infrastructure needed to convert crystals into qualified wafers and demonstrator devices. Poland has no domestic SiC boule-growth capability, and CEZAMAT does not currently operate PVT or HVPE reactors for bulk crystal growth; this is the central gap that any industrial strategy must address, most plausibly by importing capability (equipment, licensed IP, or a foreign strategic partner) rather than building it from first principles.

---

## 1. Introduction and Scope

This report assesses CEZAMAT's role in — and potential to catalyze — an advanced compound-semiconductor (III-V nitride, III-V arsenide/phosphide, and wide-bandgap SiC) crystal growth and wafer manufacturing ecosystem in Poland. It covers:

1. CEZAMAT's institutional structure, infrastructure, and consortium
2. Its documented involvement in semiconductor materials and device work, including EU Chips Act participation
3. The distinct but linked Polish GaN bulk-crystal-growth lineage (Unipress/Ammono) and how it relates to CEZAMAT
4. A technical assessment of what is present versus absent for a full crystal-growth-to-wafer pipeline
5. An economic and strategic assessment of CEZAMAT as an R&D/industrial partner
6. Recommendations and risk factors

The analysis distinguishes carefully between **CEZAMAT's own laboratories** and the broader **CEZAMAT consortium** (nine Warsaw-based research institutions under WUT leadership), since public communications sometimes elide this distinction.

## 2. Institutional Profile of CEZAMAT

### 2.1 Structure and governance

CEZAMAT is formally the Centre for Advanced Materials and Technologies at Warsaw University of Technology, positioned as WUT's flagship applied-research infrastructure. It operates as a leading multidisciplinary research and development centre in Europe, established to effectively bridge advanced scientific research with industrial implementation and commercialization, describing its mission as strengthening the country's technological sovereignty in strategic dual-use domains, with core competence in semiconductors, photonics, microelectronics technologies, as well as biotechnology and bioengineering.

Institutionally, CEZAMAT is a network of specialised laboratories distributed across several Warsaw-area host institutions rather than a single monolithic cleanroom. Its constituent laboratories include the central WUT cleanroom on Poleczki Street, a laser-nanotechnology laboratory at the Military University of Technology, a spectroscopy/magnetism laboratory at the University of Warsaw Faculty of Physics, a nitride-structures and characterization laboratory at IHPP PAS, and a photomask/micro-optics laboratory at the Institute of Electronic Materials Technology (ITME). The formal consortium — established under the CEZAMAT Project — is led by WUT and includes Institute of Physical Chemistry of the Polish Academy of Sciences, Institute of Physics of the Polish Academy of Sciences, Institute of High Pressure Physics of the Polish Academy of Sciences, Institute of Fundamental Technological Research of the Polish Academy of Sciences and other Warsaw research bodies.

This consortium structure is strategically significant: **IHPP PAS (Unipress) — Poland's actual center of bulk GaN crystal growth competence — is formally inside the CEZAMAT network**, even though its ammonothermal growth reactors are physically sited outside Warsaw proper, in Stanisławów Pierwszy.

### 2.2 Physical infrastructure and scale

CEZAMAT's central laboratory at WUT is being substantially expanded through the "Competence Centre for Microelectronics and Photonics," a National Recovery Plan (NRP)-funded joint venture with the Łukasiewicz Research Network. Under this investment, CEZAMAT WUT will purchase new technological, diagnostic and measurement equipment in the existing laboratories, and the combined technology park is intended to become a unique technology line in Poland for the manufacture of photonic integrated circuits and microelectronic devices — reportedly the only technology park in this part of Europe fully adapted to 200 mm substrates.

The stated objective of this investment is that CEZAMAT WUT will acquire new technological capabilities and competencies in semiconductor technologies, particularly in the manufacture and integration of integrated photonics components and subassemblies, as well as new generation microelectronic devices. The three-way NRP consortium is financed from National Recovery Plan (NRP) funds: Łukasiewicz Institute of Microelectronics and Photonics (leader), Łukasiewicz Institute of Tele- and Radio Technology and CEZAMAT WUT, and is framed explicitly as infrastructure to serve Polish industry: the project will directly contribute to the development of a knowledge-based economy, a model that leverages knowledge and innovation as the primary drivers of economic growth, with laboratories equipped with state-of-the-art technological infrastructure... fully meeting the requirements for conducting research in high-tech fields, particularly in semiconductor, photonic, and microelectronic technologies.

A 200 mm (8-inch) wafer-class front-end line is a materially important capability: it is the diameter class used for mainstream Si CMOS legacy nodes, a growing share of SiC power devices, and increasingly GaN-on-Si power/RF platforms. If realized as described, this would position CEZAMAT as the only 200 mm-capable facility of its kind in the Central/Eastern European region.

### 2.3 Organizational departments

CEZAMAT's administrative and technical structure includes dedicated organisational, financial and technical departments, the latter involved in securing the continuity of proper operation of the technical infrastructure, carrying out maintenance works and supervising service works. Notably, CEZAMAT also hosts a biotechnology/genomics department involved in oncology diagnostics (tumour profiling with NGS, ONT, ddPCR techniques) and projects in genomics, tanscriptonics, epigenetics, metagenomics and 3D genome structure analysis — underscoring that CEZAMAT is a multi-domain applied-research institute, not a dedicated semiconductor foundry, and that its semiconductor-relevant assets (SEMINSYS department, cleanrooms, characterization suites) are one pillar among several.

## 3. CEZAMAT's Direct Semiconductor and Compound-Semiconductor Engagement

### 3.1 The FAMES pilot line under the EU Chips Act

The single most concrete evidence of CEZAMAT's standing in advanced semiconductor manufacturing is its selection as the sole Polish participant in **FAMES**, one of the first five pilot lines funded under the EU Chips Act. Chips JU (Chips Joint Undertaking) has completed the evaluation of projects for pilot lines. The CEZAMAT WUT will be part of one of four European pilot lines for advanced semiconductor technologies. The consortium comprises leading European research and scientific entities: CEA-Leti (consortium leader), Imec, Fraunhofer Institute, Tyndall National Institute, VTT Technical Research Centre, CEZAMAT PW, Université Catholique de Louvain, Silicon Austria Lab, INP Grenoble, SiNano and University of Granada — i.e., essentially the top tier of European semiconductor RTOs (research and technology organisations), with CEZAMAT the only Central/Eastern European member.

FAMES is a large program: it is a European pilot line initiative involving 10 partners across Europe... set to run for four years with a total funding of 830 million euros, confirmed elsewhere as EUR 830 million in combined investment from the EU and participating Member States. Technically, the program targets Fully Depleted Silicon-on-Insulator (FD-SOI), a manufacturing process in which a thin insulating layer beneath each transistor allows chips to operate at significantly lower voltages, consuming 30-40% less power than conventional designs at equivalent performance, with technology nodes at FD-SOI IP Cores level and, per program materials, two new FD-SOI generation nodes at 10 nm and 7 nm along with several embedded non-volatile memory technologies.

CEZAMAT's specific technical remit within FAMES is work related to chip design, semiconductor memory technology and the demonstration of their potential in AI and chip security, including chip design and PDK module testing to provide the tools needed to design and optimize chip design and technology. CEZAMAT leadership has been explicit about how the institution frames this achievement: Professor Romuald Beck, WUT's Plenipotentiary for Strategic Projects, stated that the fact that the CEZAMAT Warsaw University of Technology has been invited to collaborate on the development of FD-SOI technology within the framework of the largest project in the history of the European Commission (European Chips Act) makes us proud... This demonstrates that CEZAMAT WUT has gained standing and international recognition of its competencies over the past few years. Piotr Wiśniewski, head of CEZAMAT's Intelligent Semiconductor Systems department (SEMINSYS), added that the FAMES project is an excellent opportunity to take advantage of the capabilities of the CEZAMAT WUT laboratories... We are the only place in Poland with such advanced research capabilities. We have highly specialized staff both in processes related to device fabrication (source truncated).

As of early 2026, FAMES became operational: FAMES Becomes First EU Chips Act Pilot Line to Reach Full Operation, formally inaugurated at CEA-Leti's Grenoble site, with CEZAMAT's Mariusz Wielec among the consortium representatives present at the inauguration ceremony. The pilot line is explicitly designed as **shared European infrastructure**: the pilot line will be accessible to all EU stakeholders (universities, RTOs, SMEs and industrial companies) and all like-minded countries through annual open calls and upon request, following a fair and non-discriminatory selection process, meaning CEZAMAT's role is partly that of a *national gateway* connecting Polish industry to a pan-European front-end fabrication capability it does not itself physically host at full technology-node depth.

**Interpretation.** FAMES demonstrates that CEZAMAT has achieved credible standing among Europe's premier semiconductor RTOs on design, PDK/memory technology, and system-level demonstration — but the pilot line's actual advanced-node FD-SOI fabrication is centered at CEA-Leti (Grenoble) and imec (Leuven). CEZAMAT's role is a downstream/design/integration role within a distributed European fab network, not an independent front-end wafer fab for advanced logic nodes. This is an important nuance for any Polish industrial strategy: CEZAMAT provides Poland *access* and *competence transfer* rather than sovereign fabrication capacity at those nodes.

### 3.2 SEMINSYS and photonics/microelectronics laboratories

CEZAMAT's semiconductor-relevant internal capability is organized substantially around the SEMINSYS (Intelligent Semiconductor Systems) department, whose leadership frames CEZAMAT as uniquely positioned nationally for advanced device-level R&D. The NRP-funded Competence Centre for Microelectronics and Photonics (Section 2.2) is intended to extend this toward the manufacture and integration of integrated photonics components and subassemblies, as well as new generation microelectronic devices, explicitly at the 200 mm substrate scale.

### 3.3 International engagement

CEZAMAT has hosted bilateral discussions with leading foreign semiconductor research institutions, including a meeting where possibilities of international cooperation, research offers and research areas of Taiwan Semiconductor Research Institute and Warsaw University of Technology were the main topics of discussion during the meeting of representatives of both institutions at the Centre for Advanced Materials and Technology CEZAMAT of Warsaw University of Technology. It has also served as a venue for defence-relevant quantum technology conferences, reflecting its dual-use orientation and defense-sector visibility. CEZAMAT is a member of SEMI, the global semiconductor industry association, which lists it as a leading multidisciplinary research and development centre in Europe operating from state-of-the-art facilities in Warsaw that enable advanced research, prototyping, technology validation and small- to medium-batch manufacturing.

## 4. Poland's Bulk Compound-Semiconductor Crystal Growth Base: IHPP PAS / Unipress

Because CEZAMAT itself is not a bulk crystal growth operation, an accurate assessment of Poland's crystal-growth ecosystem requires examining the Institute of High Pressure Physics PAS — a CEZAMAT consortium member — separately and in depth. This is, in fact, Poland's (and arguably one of the world's) most scientifically important centers for GaN bulk crystal growth.

### 4.1 Scientific origins: high-nitrogen-pressure solution growth

IHPP PAS, known as Unipress, is a leading research facility established in 1972 in Warsaw, Poland, dedicated to advancing high-pressure physics and materials engineering, having originated from the separation of an independent laboratory led by Prof. Sylwester Porowski from the Institute of Physics of the Polish Academy of Sciences. Unipress achieved the world's first high-pressure growth of GaN crystals, laying the foundation for modern optoelectronics.

The core innovation was using very high nitrogen overpressure to suppress GaN's decomposition at the temperatures required for solution growth from a Ga melt: this research led to the creation of a unique technology of high-pressure growth of GaN crystals with the dislocation density much lower than crystals grown at atmospheric pressure. This technology paved the way to construction of blue/violet laser diodes by using these crystals as substrates for growing epitaxial structures of (AlGaIn)N with exceptional structural and optical quality. The institute's own materials describe the achievement as high nitrogen pressure solution (HNPS) growth of GaN crystals with a record low dislocation density (10² cm⁻²) — several orders of magnitude below typical heteroepitaxial GaN-on-sapphire or GaN-on-SiC dislocation densities (often 10⁶–10⁹ cm⁻²).

This program was carried out under a coordinated national effort: the work on blue/violet laser diodes has been done within the Polish Government program "The development of the blue optoelectronics" coordinated by Unipress. In order to commercialize this technology, new high-tech company was founded — a reference to Ammono S.A. (discussed below) and to TopGaN, a Unipress spin-off focused on GaN laser diodes.

### 4.2 The ammonothermal (AMMONO) method and Ammono S.A.

Parallel to (and eventually converging with) the high-pressure route, Polish researchers pioneered a low(er)-pressure, high-quality alternative: ammonothermal growth. Ammono was created in 1999 by 4 Polish scientists, Robert Dwiliński, Roman Doradziński, Jerzy Garczyński, Leszek Sierzputowski, and a Japanese concern Nichia Chemical (today Nichia Corporation), with seventy percent of shares belonging to the four scientists (the founders) and 30% to Nichia; by 2010 the company employed roughly 60 people. This basic (alkaline) ammonothermal "AMMONO method" is now recognized internationally as one of the three principal industrial routes to bulk GaN, alongside HVPE and Na-flux, and is described alongside them in leading review literature as offering superior structural perfection due to near-equilibrium growth conditions.

Ammono S.A. subsequently experienced financial distress: in 2014 rehabilitation proceedings of Ammono were commenced and ended in 2015 with declaring insolvency of the company. After a period under court-appointed liquidation and interim leasing (including by Grupa Azoty Folie), the Institute of High Pressure Physics Polish Academy of Sciences (IHPP PAS) has been the leaseholder of Ammono... since the beginning of July 2017, and following a state development-agency loan, the Institute acquired Ammono S.A. in bankruptcy in 2019 and the company became part of the Institute. The commercial activity is kept as a pilot line. This is corroborated by IHPP PAS's own account: in 2019 the Institute of High Pressure Physics (IHPP) acquired Ammono S.A.

**This is a critical structural fact for any industrial strategy**: Poland's principal ammonothermal GaN bulk-crystal IP and production line is now a *state-research-institute-owned pilot line*, not an independent commercial foundry, and its commercial scale-up depends on renewed private capital, a strategic industrial partner, or public investment akin to what CEZAMAT itself received via the NRP.

### 4.3 Present state of the crystal-growth laboratory

IHPP PAS today operates GaN growth and substrate-supply activity as an ongoing pilot-scale service: we deliver GaN substrates on-demand, run out of laboratories at Stanisławów Pierwszy, Strużańska 8, in Celestynów-Lasek and in Warsaw, at Sokołowska 29/37 and Prymasa Tysiąclecia 98, with more than 250 employees at IHPP PAS, including more than 100 scientific positions and 30 PhD students, and a dedicated **theoretical group** with long-term experience in calculations and modelling of nitride semiconductors supporting process development. Institute-wide, IHPP PAS's research is formally recognized in Poland's national research-infrastructure roadmap: the Institute's research infrastructure has been included in the Polish Roadmap for Research Infrastructures as: the "GaN UNIPRESS" Centre for Nitride Semiconductor Physics and Technology, and the institute counts eight researchers from IHPP PAS in the TOP 2% of the world's most frequently cited scientists (Stanford University) list, indicating genuine scientific depth rather than purely applied/industrial activity.

Continued technology development is documented in recent device-integration publications from the institute demonstrating vertical GaN-on-GaN power diodes fabricated on ammonothermally-grown native substrates, reported as achieving high breakdown voltage and high current injection vertical GaN-on-GaN p-n diodes with extremely low on-resistance fabricated on ammonothermally grown bulk GaN substrates — precisely the kind of native-substrate power-device demonstration that differentiates true bulk GaN substrates from heteroepitaxial GaN-on-Si/SiC.

### 4.4 The CEZAMAT–Unipress link, concretely

The physical and organizational overlap is direct: IHPP PAS operates a laboratory for quantum nitride structures and advanced characterisation techniques at the Institute of High Pressure Physics (PAS), Prymasa Tysiąclecia Avenue, Warsaw as one of CEZAMAT's own constituent specialized laboratories, and separately maintains a terahertz radiation laboratory physically located *at* CEZAMAT PW's Poleczki Street site. This means CEZAMAT's laboratory network already provides a shared-infrastructure bridge between IHPP PAS's crystal-growth/materials science and CEZAMAT's device processing and characterization capability — but the bulk-growth reactors (autoclaves for ammonothermal and high-pressure GaN synthesis) themselves remain at IHPP PAS's Stanisławów Pierwszy and Celestynów-Lasek sites, outside CEZAMAT's own central cleanroom.

## 5. Technical Gap Analysis: What a Full Crystal-Growth-to-Wafer Ecosystem Requires

A vertically integrated compound-semiconductor ecosystem spans (i) raw material/precursor supply, (ii) bulk single-crystal boule growth, (iii) wafering (slicing, lapping, polishing, CMP), (iv) epitaxial layer growth (MOCVD/MBE/HVPE), (v) device fabrication, (vi) packaging/test, and (vii) qualification/reliability infrastructure. The table below maps Poland's current capability against this chain for the two most industrially relevant compound-semiconductor material systems: GaN and SiC.

| Value-chain stage | GaN (nitrides) | SiC | Institution(s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bulk boule/crystal growth | **Present** — ammonothermal (AMMONO) and high-nitrogen-pressure solution growth, pilot scale | **Absent** — no domestic PVT/sublimation SiC boule growth identified | IHPP PAS / Unipress (Ammono S.A. pilot line) |
| Wafering / substrate finishing | Present at pilot/small-batch scale (native GaN substrates supplied "on-demand") | Absent domestically | IHPP PAS |
| Epitaxy (MOCVD/MBE) | Present | Present in R&D scale via CEZAMAT/ITME-linked labs; not confirmed at industrial SiC epi scale | CEZAMAT, IHPP PAS, ITME |
| Device fabrication / cleanroom processing | Present, expanding to 200 mm class | Present in principle at CEZAMAT (process-agnostic cleanroom), not confirmed SiC-power-qualified | CEZAMAT (SEMINSYS), Łukasiewicz IMiF |
| Advanced-node digital logic (FD-SOI) | N/A (different material system) | N/A | CEZAMAT via FAMES (design/PDK/memory role; fabrication centered at CEA-Leti/imec) |
| Packaging, test, reliability qualification | Partial | Not confirmed | Fragmented across Łukasiewicz network |
| Pilot-to-volume manufacturing / fab-scale production | **Not present** — pilot lines only, no merchant compound-semiconductor fab identified | **Not present** | — |

**Key gap: SiC.** Despite SiC being the dominant near-term growth driver in the compound-semiconductor wafer market — with the global SiC wafer market variously estimated in the range of roughly **$0.5–1.1 billion (device-grade estimates) up to much larger figures in broader-scope market reports**, and unit volumes having grown from 930,000 units in 2021 to more than 1.52 million SiC wafers produced globally in 2024 — no evidence surfaced of a Polish domestic SiC boule-growth or substrate-manufacturing capability. This is consistent with the global landscape, where SiC substrate and wafer capacity remains highly concentrated: as of 2024, only seven players worldwide possessed the capability to vertically integrate across the SiC value chain, and leading capacity is anchored by players such as Wolfspeed, Coherent Corp., Infineon (Villach, Austria; Kulim, Malaysia), and Asian majors. Europe's most significant public SiC investment to date is in Germany, where Europe approved €900 million in subsidies for 9 fabrication projects, including a 120,000 wafers/year line in Germany (Wolfspeed/ZF Saarland). Poland has no comparable announced project.

**Key gap: pilot-to-volume scale-up.** Even in GaN, where Poland's IP position is genuinely strong, the Ammono/IHPP PAS operation remains explicitly a "pilot line" under state-institute ownership, supplying wafers largely on a bespoke/on-demand basis rather than at qualified, high-volume, automotive/industrial-grade throughput. The global GaN and SiC substrate industry is moving toward larger diameters (200 mm SiC now in customer rollout at Infineon and Wolfspeed; 300 mm SiC boules already demonstrated by Wolfspeed and in development at Coherent), a scale regime well beyond what a university-linked pilot autoclave line can address without substantial capital investment.

**What CEZAMAT itself does *not* appear to provide**, based on available evidence: dedicated bulk boule-growth reactors (PVT, HVPE-for-boules, ammonothermal autoclaves) within its own laboratory network; SiC-specific process infrastructure; or announced high-volume production lines. Its documented strength is squarely in **front-end wafer processing, device design/fabrication, characterization, and photonics/microelectronics integration at up to 200 mm**, plus a credible seat at the European pilot-line table via FAMES.

## 6. Economic and Strategic Assessment

### 6.1 Market context

The compound-semiconductor substrate market is entering a period of sustained, policy-amplified growth. Multiple independent market analyses converge on double-digit CAGRs for SiC wafers through the early-to-mid 2030s, variously projected at roughly 18.60% CAGR, 14.66% CAGR, or 10.5% CAGR depending on methodology and market scope, driven overwhelmingly by rapid electrification of vehicles, grid-scale rollouts of renewable energy, and 5G radio-frequency front ends alongside AI infrastructure power-delivery demand emerging as a newer driver for larger-diameter substrates. The 200 mm diameter transition — directly relevant to CEZAMAT's stated infrastructure roadmap — is described as the fastest-growing diameter segment, as the industry coalesces around the transition from 150mm to 200mm substrates as the primary near-term wafer scaling lever.

Public policy is a first-order driver of where new capacity locates. In the US, the U.S. CHIPS and Science Act funded 6 projects with combined investments of $910 million for SiC specifically, while in Europe €900 million in subsidies for 9 fabrication projects have been approved. At the pan-European level, the Chips JU's five pilot lines collectively represent €3.7 billion in European and national funding, of which FAMES alone (CEZAMAT's program) is EUR 830 million — indicating that European public capital for semiconductor scale-up is available and CEZAMAT has already demonstrated the institutional capacity to win a share of it.

The report noted above from the OECD frames the strategic stakes bluntly: the semiconductor value chain, which includes raw materials, wafer fabrication, and front-end semiconductor manufacturing, remains highly concentrated and interdependent, with a small number of countries supplying critical inputs — a concentration risk that both EU Chips Act policy and, by extension, Polish national strategy are explicitly trying to reduce.

### 6.2 CEZAMAT as R&D partner: strengths

1. **Institutional credibility at the European level.** Selection into FAMES alongside CEA-Leti, imec, and Fraunhofer is a strong, externally validated signal of technical competence and EU-level trust — not a claim CEZAMAT makes unilaterally about itself.
2. **Consortium access to genuine crystal-growth IP.** Through the CEZAMAT consortium's inclusion of IHPP PAS, a partner engaging with CEZAMAT gains an institutional pathway to world-class, IP-rich GaN bulk-crystal-growth science (record-low dislocation densities, both HNPS and ammonothermal routes) that is difficult to replicate quickly outside Japan, the US, and a handful of specialist firms.
3. **Physical infrastructure scale.** The NRP-funded 200 mm-class technology park, if delivered as planned, would be a genuinely differentiated regional asset — the only such line described as fully adapted to 200 mm substrates in this part of Europe.
4. **Multi-domain integration capacity.** CEZAMAT's simultaneous strength in photonics, microelectronics, and (separately) biotech signals an institution capable of managing complex, multi-partner, multi-funding-stream projects — a relevant organizational competence for standing up a new manufacturing ecosystem.
5. **Public funding access and track record.** Combined NRP and EU Chips Act participation demonstrates CEZAMAT's demonstrated ability to secure large-scale public co-investment, lowering the capital-formation risk for an industrial partner.

### 6.3 CEZAMAT as R&D partner: limitations and risks

1. **No independent bulk crystal growth capability.** CEZAMAT does not itself operate boule-growth reactors; a partner seeking vertically integrated crystal-to-wafer capability must engage IHPP PAS/Ammono separately (albeit within the same consortium), and that entity's crystal growth today operates at pilot rather than industrial scale.
2. **No domestic SiC capability at all.** For SiC — arguably the larger near-term commercial opportunity given automotive/EV and grid demand — Poland currently has no identified bulk growth, substrate, or wafer capacity, meaning an SiC strategy would require either building entirely new capability (high capital and technology-transfer risk) or attracting a foreign strategic investor (e.g., following the Wolfspeed–Saarland or Infineon–Villach precedents).
3. **FAMES role is design/integration, not sovereign advanced-node fabrication.** The most prestigious current program (FAMES) centers physical FD-SOI fabrication at CEA-Leti and imec; CEZAMAT's role is chip design, PDK/memory technology, and AI/security demonstration. This is valuable but should not be conflated with Poland acquiring independent advanced-node fab capacity.
4. **Capital intensity of scale-up.** Moving Ammono/IHPP PAS's GaN pilot line to industrial-volume, qualified substrate production (matching the diameter and throughput trajectory of global competitors moving to 150→200→300 mm) requires capital on a different order of magnitude than typical university/PAS-institute research budgets, and Ammono's own bankruptcy history (2014–2019) is a documented cautionary precedent for underfunded commercialization attempts.
5. **Fragmentation across many host institutions.** CEZAMAT's network model (WUT, MUT, University of Warsaw, IHPP PAS, ITME, plus Łukasiewicz partners in the newest investment) brings breadth but also coordination complexity; an industrial partner needs clarity on which entity holds IP, operational control, and commercial rights for any given process step.
6. **Verification gap on SiC-specific and volume-manufacturing claims.** Publicly available material is strong on GaN science and on FAMES, but sparse or silent on SiC-specific infrastructure, yield/qualification data, and any concrete volume-manufacturing roadmap — a due-diligence gap a prospective partner should close directly with CEZAMAT and IHPP PAS before committing capital.

### 6.4 Economic framing for a prospective industrial partner

Given the above, the economically rational role for CEZAMAT in a Polish compound-semiconductor strategy is as the **processing, integration, and European-access hub**, not as a bulk-crystal or SiC greenfield site. A plausible phased structure:

- **Near term:** Leverage CEZAMAT's FAMES membership and 200 mm cleanroom investment for GaN-on-Si or GaN-on-SiC power/RF device prototyping using externally sourced epi-ready substrates, while co-investing with IHPP PAS to scale native GaN substrate output for niche high-value applications (laser diodes, vertical power devices) where Poland's dislocation-density advantage is commercially meaningful.
- **Medium term:** Use EU Chips Act and NRP-style co-funding mechanisms — which CEZAMAT has already shown it can access — to underwrite capital equipment for Ammono/IHPP PAS scale-up (larger autoclaves, automated wafering/polishing) rather than starting a new GaN program elsewhere.
- **Long term / higher risk:** Any move into SiC bulk growth would essentially be a greenfield strategic bet requiring either licensed foreign PVT technology, a joint venture with an established SiC substrate maker, or a state-backed strategic investment comparable in scale to the German or US precedents cited above; CEZAMAT's cleanroom and characterization infrastructure could support downstream SiC device work once substrates are sourced, but should not be assumed to shorten the SiC substrate-technology acquisition timeline.

---

## 7. Conclusions

CEZAMAT is a legitimate and increasingly internationally validated node in Poland's semiconductor R&D landscape, anchored by verifiable achievements — most importantly its status as the sole Polish partner in the EU Chips Act's €830M FAMES pilot line, and its emerging 200 mm-class cleanroom infrastructure under National Recovery Plan funding. However, characterizing CEZAMAT itself as a "crystal growth" institution would overstate its role: Poland's genuine, scientifically distinguished bulk compound-semiconductor crystal growth capability — world-first high-nitrogen-pressure GaN growth and the AMMONO ammonothermal method — resides at IHPP PAS (Unipress), a CEZAMAT consortium member operating the former Ammono S.A. as a state-institute-owned pilot line since its 2019 acquisition out of bankruptcy.

For establishing an advanced compound-semiconductor crystal-growth-and-wafer ecosystem in Poland, CEZAMAT is best understood as the indispensable **processing, device-integration, and European-network hub**, while IHPP PAS/Ammono is the **crystal-growth engine**, and SiC represents an essentially unaddressed **capability gap** that neither institution currently fills. A credible national strategy — and a well-structured industrial partnership — should engage both institutions jointly, treat GaN scale-up as a nearer-term, lower-risk opportunity building on genuine IP advantage, and treat SiC as a separate, higher-capital, higher-risk undertaking requiring external technology or capital partners.
