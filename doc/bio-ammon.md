## 4. Scientific Contributions in Detail

### 4.1 The v/G Criterion and Point-Defect Engineering

One of von Ammon's most consequential technical contributions is experimental work establishing and refining the relationship between crystal **pull rate (v)** and the **axial temperature gradient (G)** at the melt–crystal interface, and how this ratio governs which type of intrinsic point defect — vacancies or silicon self-interstitials — dominates in the growing crystal. This is often referred to in the field as the **Voronkov criterion or v/G rule**, with von Ammon and coworkers (notably E. Dornberger, H. Oelkrug, and H. Weidner) providing key experimental grounding:

$$\left(\frac{v}{G}\right)_{\text{crit}} \approx 0.13\ \text{mm}^2\,\text{K}^{-1}\,\text{min}^{-1}$$

Above this critical ratio, vacancies dominate as the surviving point defect; below it, self-interstitials dominate. This threshold governs the subsequent formation of **voids (D-defects / crystal-originated pits, COPs)** on the vacancy-rich side and **dislocation loops (A-/B-swirl defects)** on the interstitial-rich side — both of which are yield-limiting defects in device fabrication. Their 1995 paper in *Journal of Crystal Growth* (von Ammon, Dornberger, Oelkrug, Weidner) is cited as foundational supporting evidence for this criterion in subsequent literature, including the influential 2003 *Angewandte Chemie* review by Evers, Klüfers, Staudigl, and Stallhofer.

### 4.2 Defect Engineering Review (Sinno, Dornberger, von Ammon, Brown & Dupret, 2000)

Von Ammon is a co-author of "**Defect Engineering of Czochralski Single-Crystal Silicon**" (*Materials Science and Engineering: R*, vol. 28, 2000, pp. 149–198), a highly cited, comprehensive review co-authored with T. Sinno, E. Dornberger, R. A. Brown, and F. Dupret. This review consolidates the theoretical and industrial understanding of point-defect aggregation, oxygen precipitation, and their coupling to crystal-growth process parameters, and is frequently cited in the crystal-growth-modeling literature (e.g., in *Annual Review* articles on bulk crystal growth modeling) as a standard reference in the field.

### 4.3 Ring-Like Defect Distributions and Stacking Faults

With E. Dornberger, von Ammon investigated the radial distribution of **oxidation-induced stacking faults (OSF rings)** in CZ silicon and their dependence on pull rate and axial temperature gradient, contributing to the predictive modeling of where in a wafer these defects will occur — work published in outlets including the *Journal of the Electrochemical Society*.

### 4.4 Magnetic Czochralski (MCZ) Growth

Von Ammon contributed to the development and industrial application of magnetically stabilized CZ growth, including:
- Static **cusp**, **horizontal (transverse)**, and **axial** magnetic-field configurations to damp turbulent melt convection and control oxygen incorporation.
- **Dynamic ("traveling") magnetic fields**, a technology associated with Wacker's process development, which actively drive a controlled convection pattern in the melt rather than merely suppressing turbulence — analogous in physical principle to linear-motor/magnetic-levitation drive systems.

He is listed as a co-author on conference proceedings addressing the application of magnetic fields in industrial silicon growth (e.g., with Y. Gelfgat, L. Gorbunov, A. Muhlbauer, A. Muiznieks, Y. Makarov, J. Virbulis, and G. Müller, presented at joint Riga–PAMIR MHD conferences), reflecting sustained collaboration with magnetohydrodynamics specialists to bring this physics into production furnace design.

### 4.5 Oxygen Behavior, Thermal Donors, and Internal Gettering

Building on the foundational v/G and defect-engineering work, von Ammon's group addressed the practical control of **interstitial oxygen** incorporated from the silica (SiO₂) crucible into the melt and crystal — governing:
- **Thermal donor formation** (oxygen-related electrically active defects affecting wafer resistivity),
- **Oxygen precipitation** during wafer thermal processing, engineered to create a **denuded zone (DZ)** near the wafer surface (a low-defect region for active devices) while retaining **internal gettering (IG)** sites in the bulk to trap harmful transition-metal contaminants (Fe, Cu, Ni).
