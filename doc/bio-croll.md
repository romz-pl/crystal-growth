# Arne Cröll — Scientific Biography

## Overview

Arne Cröll is a German crystallographer and materials scientist, born in 1957, who holds the Chair of Crystallography at the Albert-Ludwigs-Universität Freiburg (University of Freiburg), where he has served since December 2003. His research centers on the growth and solidification of semiconductor and other functional crystals, with a particular emphasis on the role of fluid flow (thermocapillary, solutocapillary, and buoyancy-driven convection) in determining crystal quality and defect formation. He is one of the most experienced researchers worldwide in the use of microgravity (space- and drop-based) experiments to isolate and study convective transport phenomena during crystal growth, and in later career phases has extended this expertise into high-temperature materials testing for nuclear thermal propulsion fuels.

## Research Fields and Expertise

Cröll's work spans several closely related areas of crystal growth science and materials characterization:

1. **Melt growth and solidification of semiconductor crystals** — Floating-zone (FZ), Czochralski, Bridgman, and Vertical Gradient Freeze (VGF) techniques applied to silicon, germanium, germanium-silicon (SiGe) alloys, GaAs, GaSb, and related compound semiconductors.
2. **Fluid flow and convection in crystal growth melts** — Thermocapillary (Marangoni) convection, solutocapillary convection, thermoelectromagnetic convection (TEMC), and buoyancy-driven flow, and their influence on dopant striations, segregation, and defect formation.
3. **Convection control techniques** — Application of static, rotating (RMF), and travelling magnetic fields; mechanical and acoustic vibrations; and detached (crucible-free) growth configurations to suppress or modify melt convection.
4. **Microgravity crystal growth** — Extensive design, execution, and analysis of crystal growth experiments flown on sounding rockets (TEXUS, MAXUS), parabolic flights, and orbital missions (Spacelab, FOTON-M3, FOTON-M4, International Space Station), used as a means of studying diffusive versus convective transport in the near-absence of buoyancy convection.
5. **Detached Bridgman growth** — A crucible-free variant of the Bridgman method that reduces mechanical stress and dislocation density in grown crystals; Cröll is a recognized authority on this technique (co-author of the widely cited MRS Bulletin review "Detached Bridgman Growth—A Standard Crystal Growth Method with a New Twist").
6. **Particle–interface interactions during solidification** — Engulfment versus pushing of second-phase particles (e.g., SiC, Si₃N₄) at the solid–liquid interface during directional solidification of multicrystalline silicon, relevant to photovoltaic-grade silicon production.
7. **Photovoltaic (solar) silicon** — Directional solidification of multicrystalline silicon, crucible-coating impurity control, carbon and dopant segregation, and defect reduction for solar cell applications.
8. **Materials characterization** — Wetting angle and surface tension measurements of semiconductor melts (Ge, Ge-Si, Ga-GaSb-Sb) on various substrates; X-ray diffraction imaging/topography (including synchrotron-based, real-time, high-temperature in-situ studies) for dislocation dynamics and wafer metrology; thermophysical property measurement (thermal conductivity, specific heat) of crystals such as PbI₂ and InI.
9. **Crystal growth of functional/detector materials** — Lead iodide (PbI₂) and indium(I) iodide (InI) single crystals for X-ray/γ-ray detection, tin monosulfide (SnS) for photovoltaic absorber applications.
10. **High-temperature nuclear materials (later career, via UAH/NASA MSFC)** — Hot-hydrogen exposure testing and characterization of uranium- and zirconium-based carbide, nitride, and carbonitride nuclear fuel forms (e.g., (U,Zr)C, UN, ZrC, NbC, (ZrNbW)C) for Nuclear Thermal Propulsion (NTP) reactor fuel development, including mass-loss behavior, protective refractory-metal barrier coatings, and thermophysical characterization under extreme conditions.

## Space and Microgravity Missions

Cröll has been involved in crystal growth experiments across numerous microgravity platforms over more than three decades, including:
- **Sounding rocket missions** (TEXUS, MAXUS series — e.g., MAXUS 4 floating-zone silicon growth experiments studying vibration- and rotating-magnetic-field-controlled convection)
- **Spacelab missions**
- **FOTON-M3** (2007) and **FOTON-M4** (2014) — unmanned Russian recoverable capsules, used for Bridgman and floating-zone growth of germanium, germanium-silicon, and gallium-doped germanium/antimonide crystals, including detached Bridgman growth studies and rotating-magnetic-field experiments
- **International Space Station (ISS)** — detached and floating-zone growth of semiconductor crystals, and particle engulfment/pushing studies for silicon under near-diffusive microgravity conditions

Freiburg Crystallography, under his leadership, has participated in microgravity crystal growth experiments for several decades, in collaboration with the German Aerospace Center (DLR) and the European Space Agency (ESA).

## Biographical Note (Personal)

Outside his scientific career, Cröll is also an accomplished fine-art photographer, working primarily in traditional silver-gelatin black-and-white large- and medium-format film photography (view cameras from 4×5" to 8×10"), a pursuit he has followed seriously since around 1981. His photographic training included workshops with Peter Gasser, the Zone VI workshops, Linhof, John Sexton, and Michael A. Smith and Paula Chamlee. His "Crystalscapes" gallery—photomicrographs of patterns found in his own scientific crystal samples—directly bridges his professional and artistic work.
