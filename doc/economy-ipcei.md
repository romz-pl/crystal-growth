# Important Projects of Common European Interest (IPCEIs) and the Strengthening of Europe's Semiconductor Bulk Crystal Growth and Wafer Manufacturing Industry

*A technical and policy analysis, with emphasis on implications for Poland*

---

## Executive Summary

Europe's semiconductor materials base — bulk crystal growth of silicon, silicon carbide (SiC), gallium nitride (GaN), and III‑V compounds, together with the downstream wafering, epitaxy, and substrate-finishing operations that convert boules into device-ready wafers — sits at a structurally exposed point in the global chip supply chain. The European Union holds roughly 10% of global semiconductor manufacturing capacity while consuming a much larger share of output, and it is a net importer of high-purity polysilicon, SiC substrates, and compound-semiconductor materials. The Important Projects of Common European Interest (IPCEI) instrument — a State-aid derogation under Article 107(3)(b) TFEU that allows Member States to jointly fund large, high-risk, cross-border industrial projects that "the market alone" would not deliver — has become the EU's principal vehicle for closing this gap without breaching ordinary State-aid discipline.

Two IPCEIs in microelectronics have already been approved (2018 and 2023), a third — IPCEI on Advanced Semiconductor Technologies (IPCEI AST) — is in structuring as of mid-2026, and all sit alongside the European Chips Act's grant and "First-of-a-Kind" facility mechanisms. Materials and crystal-growth capacity — wide-bandgap substrates (SiC, GaN), compound semiconductors (III‑V), and associated epitaxy — is one of the five technology pillars of the original IPCEI Microelectronics and one of the four workstreams of IPCEI ME/CT (specifically "ACT," covering power electronics). This report examines how the IPCEI framework is structured, how it has been and is being used to fund crystal-growth and wafer capability across the Union, and what this means concretely for establishing or scaling crystal-growth facilities in Poland, which is a full participant in IPCEI ME/CT and is developing complementary national instruments.

## 1. The IPCEI Legal and Policy Framework

### 1.1 Legal basis

IPCEIs are authorised under **Article 107(3)(b) of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU)**, which permits State aid to "promote the execution of an important project of common European interest." The European Commission's **2021 IPCEI Communication** sets out the cumulative eligibility criteria a proposed IPCEI must satisfy:

- **Common European interest**: the project must contribute to EU strategic objectives (e.g., digital and green transition, technological sovereignty) and involve at least four Member States, with benefits extending beyond the direct participants.
- **Significant contribution**: the project must be highly innovative or represent a major first-of-a-kind industrial deployment, going beyond the current state of the art in the sector.
- **Involvement of multiple Member States**: co-financing and cross-border collaboration are structural requirements, not optional add-ons.
- **Private co-financing and clear positive spillovers**: participants must generate knowledge diffusion to the wider European ecosystem, not just to consortium members.
- **Market-failure justification**: financing must not be available under normal market conditions because of the technological risk, capital intensity, or long payback horizon — precisely the profile of bulk crystal growth and wafer fabrication.

Unlike ordinary State aid, an IPCEI allows aid intensities to approach 100% of the *funding gap* (the difference between the costs of the project and the standalone profitability the market would fund), rather than being capped by the general regional or R&D&I aid ceilings. This is the central reason IPCEIs are the preferred vehicle for capital-intensive, high-risk materials and crystal-growth projects: conventional aid schemes cannot cover the scale of investment a crystal-growth pilot line or first industrial deployment requires.

### 1.2 The two-track structure: R&D&I vs. First Industrial Deployment (FID)

A defining structural feature of IPCEIs relevant to crystal growth is the distinction between:

- **Research, Development and Innovation (R&D&I)** activities, typically up to Technology Readiness Level (TRL) 7–8; and
- **First Industrial Deployment (FID)**, the initial industrial-scale roll-out of a technology that has not previously been deployed at that scale in Europe.

Crystal growth of new substrate generations — 200 mm SiC boules, 300 mm silicon for advanced logic, GaN-on-Si epitaxial stacks — is exactly the kind of activity that straddles this boundary: process development is R&D, but building and qualifying the first production-representative growth furnace or CVD/PVT reactor line is FID. IPCEIs are explicitly designed to fund *both* stages within a single notified project, which is what distinguishes them from Horizon Europe or purely national R&D grants.

### 1.3 What IPCEIs cannot do

It is important to state the limitation clearly, because it shapes national co-financing strategy (see Section 5 on Poland): **IPCEIs cannot fund pure mass-production capacity expansion.** As the German federal government's own IPCEI ME/CT documentation states plainly, "it is not possible to fund capacity expansion for mass production in an IPCEI." IPCEI funding covers R&D&I and FID up to initial industrial-scale qualification; subsequent capacity ramp-up for volume manufacturing must be financed by private investment, national investment-aid schemes, or — since 2023 — the European Chips Act's grant instruments and the "European Chips Fund." This handoff from IPCEI-funded FID to Chips-Act-funded capacity scaling is the operative model for how a crystal-growth line moves from pilot status to a commercial fab.

## 2. IPCEI Microelectronics I (2018) and the Materials/Compound-Semiconductor Pillar

The first Microelectronics IPCEI was approved by the European Commission on 18 December 2018, with Austria joining a subsequent extension in March 2021. It ultimately involved 32 companies and research and technology organisations (RTOs) from four Member States (France, Germany, Italy, Austria) plus the United Kingdom, organised around **five technology fields**:

1. Energy-efficient chips
2. Power semiconductors
3. Sensors
4. Advanced optical equipment
5. **Compound materials**

The compound-materials pillar is the direct institutional ancestor of today's crystal-growth and wide-bandgap-substrate funding. It supported early European capability in SiC and GaN materials at a time when substrate supply for power electronics was overwhelmingly dependent on non-European (principally US and Japanese) producers. Bosch's SiC pilot line — which began IPCEI-I operations at roughly 35 wafer starts per day with plans to double capacity annually — is one of the clearest crystal-growth-adjacent outcomes of this first wave, demonstrating the IPCEI-to-scale-up pathway that later projects have tried to replicate and accelerate.

## 3. IPCEI ME/CT (2023): The Current Backbone Instrument

### 3.1 Scale and structure

The European Commission approved the second microelectronics IPCEI — **IPCEI on Microelectronics and Communication Technologies (IPCEI ME/CT)** — on 8 June 2023. It was jointly notified by **fourteen Member States**: Austria, Czechia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Ireland, Italy, Malta, the Netherlands, **Poland**, Romania, Slovakia, and Spain, with a further five Member States (Belgium, Hungary, Latvia, Portugal, Slovenia) and Norway participating as associated partners.

Key figures:
- Up to **€8.1 billion** in public funding across the fourteen notifying states.
- Expected to unlock a further **€13.7 billion** in private investment (some sources cite figures up to €20+ billion when combined with the first IPCEI's leverage).
- **56 companies** (including SMEs and start-ups) undertaking **68 projects**, plus over 30 associated participants and more than 180 planned cross-border collaborations.
- Motto: *"Safety, Security, Sustainability and Sovereignty."*
- Several Member States (including Austria, Czechia, Finland, France, Germany, Italy, Latvia, Romania, Slovakia, and Spain) route part of their national co-financing through the EU **Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF)**, with all RRF-funded IPCEI ME/CT activities scheduled to conclude in 2026.

### 3.2 The four workstreams

IPCEI ME/CT organises its 68 projects into four thematic workstreams spanning the full value chain "from materials and tools to chip designs and production processes":

| Workstream | Focus | Relevance to crystal growth / wafers |
|---|---|---|
| **THINK** | AI-enabled processors | Downstream of substrates; drives demand for high-purity silicon and advanced-node wafers |
| **SENSE** | Sensors and optoelectronics | Compound-semiconductor substrates (III-V, SiC), photonic integrated circuits |
| **COMMUNICATE** | High-frequency electronics, communications | GaN-on-SiC RF substrates, compound epitaxy |
| **ACT** | Power electronics | **Direct crystal-growth and bulk-substrate scaling: 200 mm SiC, integrated GaN power devices, thin 300 mm Power MOSFETs** |

The **ACT** workstream is the clearest through-line from the IPCEI framework to bulk crystal growth in the strict materials-science sense. Infineon's ACT sub-projects, for example, explicitly target **Power SiC 200 mm** substrate and device scaling, **integrated Power GaN 200 mm**, and **ultra-thin Power MOSFETs at 300 mm** — i.e., moving European wide-bandgap material production from the 150 mm (6-inch) wafers that dominate today toward the 200 mm (8-inch) diameter that is becoming the industry's cost-competitive standard.

### 3.3 Representative projects with materials/crystal-growth content

- **Infineon (Germany/Austria)** — Power SiC 200 mm, integrated Power GaN 200 mm, ultra-thin Power MOSFETs 300 mm (ACT workstream).
- **ams OSRAM (Germany)** — "OptoSuRe" project: R&D for next-generation optoelectronic compound-semiconductor devices and processes, funded by the German federal government and the Free State of Bavaria over 2023–2027, including new front-end/back-end compound-semiconductor process lines.
- **Fondazione Bruno Kessler / FBK (Italy)** — expanding SiC and germanium-carbide process lines and building a dedicated SiC cleanroom, with new demonstrator devices in SiC, Ge, and diamond-based materials.
- **NXP Semiconductors** — participating in three of the four workstreams (SENSE, THINK, COMMUNICATE) across Austria, Germany, the Netherlands, and Romania, explicitly positioned as complementary to NXP's role in the ESMC joint venture with TSMC, Bosch, and Infineon (Dresden foundry) — illustrating how IPCEI-funded materials/design R&D and Chips-Act-funded manufacturing capacity are meant to interlock.
- **VIGO Photonics (Poland)** — the **HyperPIC** project (mid-infrared photonic integrated circuits), which is directly relevant to Section 5 below.

## 4. IPCEI Advanced Semiconductor Technologies (IPCEI AST): The Emerging Third Pillar

As of mid-2026, the Commission and Member States are structuring a **third** microelectronics-family IPCEI, **IPCEI Advanced Semiconductor Technologies (IPCEI AST)**, listed within the Commission's "Design Support Hub" mechanism that assists Member States in structuring new IPCEIs.

According to the Council of the European Union's June 2026 document on the matter, IPCEI AST is intended to:

- Address Europe's **"chronic structural weaknesses in design and production"** across the semiconductor and quantum-technology value chain.
- Cover a wide technology scope: AI chips and accelerators, photonic integrated circuits, heterogeneous integration and advanced packaging, sensors, power electronics, energy-efficient solutions, and secure communications — together with enabling technologies including **materials**, EDA, equipment, and testing.
- Be explicitly coordinated with the **European Chips Act** and its successor policy package, given that IPCEIs alone are judged — by the Council's own assessment — insufficient to address capacity building in the form of **pilot lines and shared design infrastructures**, which require Union-level, rather than purely consortium-level, financing due to their scale.

This is a significant acknowledgment at Council level: two rounds of microelectronics IPCEIs have strengthened R&D&I and FID capability, but large-scale pilot-line infrastructure — the kind of shared, third-party-accessible crystal-growth and wafer-processing pilot facility that a country such as Poland might host — is understood to require Chips-Act-style Union funding *in addition to* IPCEI co-financing, not IPCEI funding alone. For any Member State weighing a new crystal-growth facility, this means the realistic funding architecture is **blended**: IPCEI State aid for the R&D&I/FID component, Chips Act grants or the European Chips Fund for pilot-line and capacity-scaling components, plus national instruments and private capital.

## 5. Poland in the IPCEI Framework: Status and Strategic Position

### 5.1 Formal participation

Poland is one of the fourteen Member States that jointly notified IPCEI ME/CT in 2023, placing it inside the formal governance and funding structure of the current microelectronics IPCEI generation — not merely an associated bystander. Poland is also a signatory to earlier cross-border microelectronics declarations dating to the December 2020 "Declaration on a European Initiative on Processors and Semiconductor Technologies," and is a member of the **Chips Joint Undertaking**.

### 5.2 The national instrument: Framework for Supporting Strategic Semiconductor Investments

Poland's Ministry of Digital Affairs has proposed a **"Framework for Supporting Strategic Semiconductor Investments"** with an intended budget of roughly **$1.5 billion**, designed specifically to complement IPCEI participation. Its stated design:

- Covers the **entire semiconductor value chain**, from design through to production capacity — explicitly including the crystal-growth-to-wafer segment that pure IPCEI funding cannot fully cover for mass production.
- Requires investors to first obtain **European Commission recognition** of their project (i.e., alignment with IPCEI or Chips Act frameworks) before accessing Polish public aid, ensuring the national scheme operates as a complement to, not a substitute for, EU-level instruments.
- Is oriented toward investments that enhance **EU-wide competitiveness**, sustainable growth, and domestic job creation — the standard "spillover" justification the Commission requires of IPCEI-adjacent national aid.

This structure directly addresses the funding gap identified in Section 4: IPCEI ME/CT funds Polish participants' R&D&I and FID activity, while the national framework is designed to backstop the subsequent capacity-scaling phase that IPCEI rules exclude.

### 5.3 Polish industrial base relevant to crystal growth and materials

- **VIGO Photonics** (Ożarów Mazowiecki, near Warsaw) is the clearest Polish node directly engaged in bulk crystal growth and epitaxy. VIGO operates a complete production line spanning **crystal growth, epitaxy (both MBE and MOCVD), processing, and packaging** for III-V compound semiconductors (GaAs, InP substrates), targeting infrared detectors, VCSEL laser diodes, and — via its **HyperPIC** project — mid-infrared photonic integrated circuits. HyperPIC is formally recognised within IPCEI ME/CT, has received conditional financing from the European Investment Bank, and is eligible for Poland's **FENG** (Fundusze Europejskie dla Nowoczesnej Gospodarki) funds specifically because of its IPCEI status. VIGO's semiconductor-materials segment revenue grew 42% between 2023 and 2025, alongside strong growth in its military/defence detector business — indicating the crystal-growth/epitaxy segment is commercially self-reinforcing, not solely grant-dependent.
- **Multinational back-end presence**: Intel Technology Poland, Infineon Technologies Poland, STMicroelectronics Polska, NXP Semiconductors Poland, and Texas Instruments Poland all operate in-country, primarily in assembly, test, and design roles rather than bulk crystal growth — but this footprint provides the downstream demand base, skilled workforce, and logistics infrastructure (Lower Silesia's technology corridor linking Western design hubs to Eastern manufacturing) that would support co-locating upstream materials/wafer capacity.
- **Membership in the "Semicon Coalition"**, a broader ~€43 billion European financing framework, gives Poland preferential access to cross-border pilot lines and priority wafer allocation from the planned Dresden mega-fab (ESMC/TSMC-Bosch-Infineon-NXP joint venture) as production ramps from 2026 — relevant context for any Polish crystal-growth facility, since a domestic substrate source would reduce dependency on that single allocation.

### 5.4 Implications for establishing or expanding crystal-growth facilities in Poland

Drawing on the funding architecture above, several concrete implications follow for a prospective Polish bulk-crystal-growth or wafer facility (silicon, SiC, or III-V):

1. **Funding-gap eligibility**: A Polish crystal-growth project (e.g., a SiC boule-growth and wafering line, or expansion of VIGO's III-V compound capability) can in principle be structured as a new project *within* an existing IPCEI workstream (most naturally ACT for power/SiC, or SENSE for compound/optoelectronic materials) or as a candidate project under the emerging IPCEI AST, provided it meets the "significant contribution beyond state of the art" and cross-border spillover tests.
2. **Two-stage financing is mandatory, not optional**: Because IPCEI funding cannot cover mass-production capacity, any facility beyond pilot/FID scale must pair IPCEI aid with (a) Poland's national Framework for Supporting Strategic Semiconductor Investments, and/or (b) European Chips Act grant instruments and the European Chips Fund, and/or (c) private capital — mirroring the Bosch and Infineon SiC scale-up pattern from IPCEI-I.
3. **Commission pre-clearance is a hard gate**: The Polish national framework explicitly requires investors to secure EU recognition of the project before Polish state aid is disbursed — meaning early, well-documented engagement with the Commission's Design Support Hub (the same mechanism structuring IPCEI AST) is a practical prerequisite for any Polish crystal-growth investment seeking public co-financing.
4. **Workforce and RTO ecosystem**: Existing Polish participation spans universities, RTOs, and industrial partners already networked into the IPCEI ME/CT's 180+ planned cross-border collaborations; a new crystal-growth facility would plausibly draw on this existing consortium relationship rather than starting cold, particularly for process characterisation, defect metrology, and simulation work (the modelling side — Czochralski/Bridgman/VGF simulation, melt-convection and segregation analysis — sits naturally alongside RTO partners already inside the ecosystem).
5. **Strategic rationale beyond economics**: Council-level framing of IPCEI AST as addressing "chronic structural weaknesses" and the explicit linkage to "Safety, Security, Sustainability and Sovereignty" gives a Central European Member State a strong policy argument for hosting upstream materials capacity — diversifying the Union's crystal-growth footprint away from concentration in Germany, France, and Italy, and reducing the single-point dependency risk on any one mega-fab's wafer allocation (e.g., Dresden).
6. **Timing window**: With RRF-funded IPCEI ME/CT activity concluding in 2026 and IPCEI AST still in structuring, 2026–2027 is the practical window in which a Polish crystal-growth proposal could be incorporated into IPCEI AST's initial project list, rather than needing to wait for a fourth IPCEI round.

## 6. Synthesis: How the IPCEI Framework Functions as an Industrial-Policy Pipeline for Crystal Growth

Read together, the three generations of microelectronics IPCEIs describe a coherent, if incomplete, pipeline:

- **IPCEI Microelectronics I (2018–)** established the compound-materials and power-semiconductor pillars and proved the FID-to-scale-up model (Bosch SiC pilot line → capacity doubling).
- **IPCEI ME/CT (2023–2026, RRF-linked)** broadened participation to fourteen Member States including Poland, formalised the ACT workstream's explicit 200 mm SiC / 200 mm GaN / 300 mm thin-MOSFET targets, and drew national companies (VIGO Photonics) into EU-recognised materials projects with EIB co-financing.
- **IPCEI AST (in structuring, 2026–)** is designed to close the acknowledged gap between R&D&I/FID funding and shared pilot-line/design-infrastructure capacity, explicitly coordinated with the Chips Act, and covering materials as an enabling technology alongside AI chips, photonics, and packaging.

For Poland specifically, the combination of (i) formal IPCEI ME/CT membership, (ii) a purpose-built national co-financing instrument requiring EU recognition as a gate, (iii) an existing, commercially growing compound-semiconductor crystal-growth operator (VIGO Photonics) already inside the IPCEI ecosystem, and (iv) a Council-level policy narrative favouring geographic diversification of Europe's materials base, together constitute a credible — though not yet fully capitalised — pathway to establishing or expanding bulk crystal-growth capacity domestically. The principal open variable is whether a Polish project secures a place in IPCEI AST's initial cohort and whether Chips Act-linked capacity funding materialises on a timeline that matches the 2026 conclusion of RRF-funded ME/CT activity.
