# Scientific Biography: Erich (Evgenii) Dornberger

## Overview

Erich Dornberger (also transliterated/rendered as Evgenii Dornberger) is a German industrial scientist and technology director whose career has been built almost entirely within the Wacker group of companies — spanning **Wacker Siltronic AG** (silicon single-crystal growth) and **Wacker Chemie AG / Wacker Polysilicon** (polysilicon feedstock production). He holds a PhD and is best known for foundational contributions to the theory and industrial control of **intrinsic point-defect dynamics in Czochralski (CZ) silicon crystal growth** — work that helped transform CZ silicon production from an empirical craft into a physically modeled, defect-engineered industrial process. He subsequently moved into leadership of polysilicon feedstock technology (Siemens-process rods and fluidized-bed-reactor granular polysilicon) at Wacker, holding the title of Director.

## Institutional Affiliation

- **Wacker Siltronic AG** (Burghausen, Germany) — R&D scientist/engineer, Crystal Growth group, working alongside Wilfried von Ammon and colleagues on Czochralski silicon crystal growth process and defect physics.
- **Wacker Chemie AG / Wacker Polysilicon** (Burghausen, Germany, and later Nünchritz) — later career role as Director, Polysilicon, overseeing feedstock technology including Siemens-process polysilicon rods and fluidized-bed-reactor (FBR) granular polysilicon.

Both organizations sit within the broader Wacker corporate group, and Dornberger's career reflects a consistent trajectory: from crystal-growth defect physics (the "downstream" single-crystal process) to polysilicon feedstock manufacturing (the "upstream" raw-material process) — the two ends of the silicon production chain that Wacker has historically integrated.

## Scientific Contributions

### 1. Point-defect dynamics and the Voronkov (v/G) criterion

Dornberger's core scientific legacy lies in the experimental and computational elaboration of intrinsic point-defect behavior in CZ silicon, building directly on Vladimir Voronkov's 1982 theoretical framework for vacancy/self-interstitial recombination near the growth interface (the **v/G criterion**, where *v* is the pull rate and *G* the axial temperature gradient at the interface). Together with Wilfried von Ammon and coworkers, Dornberger helped establish the *experimental* backbone of this theory in silicon crystals grown at industrial scale:

- **von Ammon, Dornberger, Oelkrug, Weidner** (1995), *"The dependence of bulk defects on the axial temperature gradient of silicon crystals during Czochralski growth,"* *J. Cryst. Growth* 151, 273 — established the empirical dependence of grown-in microdefects on the axial thermal gradient $G$, a cornerstone reference for the field.
- **Dornberger, von Ammon** (1996), *"The dependence of ring-like distributed stacking faults on the axial temperature gradient of growing Czochralski silicon crystals,"* *J. Electrochem. Soc.* 143, 1648–1653 — quantitatively linked the radial position of the oxidation-induced stacking-fault (OSF) ring to the local $v/G$ ratio, giving crystal growers a practical diagnostic for point-defect regime.
- **Sinno, Brown, von Ammon, Dornberger** (1998), *"Point defect dynamics and the oxidation-induced stacking-fault ring in Czochralski-grown silicon crystals,"* *J. Electrochem. Soc.* 145, 302–318 — coupled Dornberger's experimental OSF-ring data with continuum point-defect transport models developed at MIT (Brown group), forming one of the field's most-cited defect-engineering papers.
- **Dornberger, Gräf, Suhren, Lambert, Wagner, Dupret, von Ammon** (1997), *J. Cryst. Growth* 180, 343 — further refined the coupling between thermal-field simulation and measured defect distributions.
- **Dornberger, von Ammon, Virbulis, Hanna, Sinno** (2001), *"Modeling of transient point defect dynamics in Czochralski silicon crystals,"* *J. Cryst. Growth* 230, 291–299 — extended the framework from steady-state to transient growth conditions (e.g., abrupt pull-rate changes), showing how large interstitial reservoirs can form near the interface after rate transients — directly relevant to industrial process control during diameter/shoulder transitions.

These papers are extensively cited in the subsequent defect-engineering literature (Voronkov, Falster, Sinno, Vanhellemont, Kalaev, and others), and Dornberger and von Ammon's OSF-ring/$v$–$G$ dataset is treated as a canonical experimental reference against which numerical point-defect models continue to be benchmarked, including in 21st-century work on 300 mm crystal growth.

### 2. Coupling of global heat/melt-flow simulation with defect modeling

Dornberger's work sat at the interface between industrial process engineering and computational crystal-growth modeling. He collaborated with the global thermal/melt-convection simulation community around Wacker Siltronic — notably with **Juris Virbulis**, **Andris Muiznieks**, **Ekkehard Tomzig**, **Wilfried von Ammon**, and Russian CFD specialists **Vladimir V. Kalaev** and **Evgenii M. Smirnov** (STR Group / Ioffe Institute / St. Petersburg Polytechnic) — to connect global-scale melt/crystal thermal-field and turbulent-convection simulations to the local $v/G$ conditions at the growth interface that determine point-defect incorporation:

- **Virbulis, Wetzel, Muiznieks, Hanna, Dornberger, Tomzig, Mühlbauer, von Ammon** (2001), *J. Cryst. Growth* 230, 92.
- **Ivanov, Smirnov, Dornberger, Virbulis, Tomzig, von Ammon** (2001), *J. Cryst. Growth* 230, 22.
- **Evstratov, Kalaev, Zhmakin, Makarov, Abramov, Ivanov, Korsakov, Smirnov, Dornberger, Virbulis, Tomzig, von Ammon** (2002), *J. Cryst. Growth* 237–239, 1757.

This body of work is a recurring reference point in review literature on numerical modeling of bulk silicon crystal growth (e.g., Miller & Friedrich's 2010 *physica status solidi (b)* review, and Sinno, Dornberger, von Ammon, Brown & Dupret's own review, below).

### 3. Landmark review article: "Defect engineering of Czochralski single-crystal silicon"

Dornberger is a named co-author of one of the field's most heavily cited review papers:

> **Sinno, T., Dornberger, E., von Ammon, W., Brown, R.A., Dupret, F.** (2000). *"Defect engineering of Czochralski single-crystal silicon."* *Materials Science and Engineering: R: Reports*, 28(5), 149–198.

This review, co-authored across MIT (Sinno, Brown), Wacker Siltronic (Dornberger, von Ammon), and Université catholique de Louvain (Dupret), synthesized a decade of industrial and academic progress in point-defect and microdefect engineering and remains a standard reference cited across essentially all subsequent silicon defect-engineering literature, including 2020s-era reviews (e.g., in *Annual Review of Chemical and Biomolecular Engineering*, "Modeling the Growth of Bulk Single Crystals: Seeing What Is Hidden").

### 4. Boron doping and dopant effects on defect distribution

Dornberger (with von Ammon) also investigated how heavy dopant concentrations shift point-defect incorporation, work with direct industrial relevance to heavily boron-doped substrates:

- **Dornberger, von Ammon**, *"Influence of boron concentration on the oxidation-induced stacking fault ring in Czochralski silicon crystals"* — showed that boron doping above ~10¹⁷ cm⁻³ shifts the R-OSF ring toward the wafer center without any change in growth parameters, and derived a boron-dependent critical ratio $C_{crit}(C_B)$, linking crystal-originated particle (COP) and gate-oxide-integrity (GOI) yield distributions to the R-OSF pattern.

### 5. Multi-crystalline silicon seeding and feedstock-quality studies

Bridging his crystal-growth and feedstock-technology careers, Dornberger conducted comparative studies of different silicon feedstock types — Single Crystalline Crushed (SCS), Fluidized-Bed-Reactor (FBR), and Siemens (SIE) feedstock — as seeding layers for growing cylindrical, high-performance multicrystalline ingots (~1.2 kg), systematically varying seed particle size and correlating it with ingot crystallinity and performance — directly relevant to photovoltaic-grade multicrystalline silicon production.

### 6. Later career: polysilicon feedstock technology

As Director of Polysilicon at Wacker Chemie AG, Dornberger's later publications and patents shift from crystal-growth defect physics to the technology of polysilicon feedstock manufacturing itself, spanning both established Siemens-process trichlorosilane deposition and newer fluidized-bed-reactor (FBR) granular polysilicon production:

- Work (with **E. Schindlbeck**) on polysilicon feedstock supply/demand dynamics for the photovoltaic industry, including analysis of how the 2008–2009 global financial crisis and changes to Spain's feed-in-tariff policy affected polysilicon demand forecasts that had previously assumed feedstock scarcity as the binding constraint on PV industry growth.
- Reporting on the construction and start-up of a pilot plant for granular polysilicon production via fluidized-bed reactor technology, including achievement of steady-state process conditions and stable particle-size distribution — a step toward full-scale FBR production at Wacker Polysilicon.
- Continued involvement in point-defect thermophysical-parameter estimation, co-authoring later work on the challenge of determining globally robust thermophysical property estimates for intrinsic point defects in crystalline silicon, noting that prior single-dataset regression approaches had failed to produce parameter sets predictive across multiple experimental conditions.

## Patents

Dornberger holds numerous patents (assigned to Wacker Siltronic AG / Siltronic AG), reflecting the direct industrial application of his research. Representative examples:

- **DE69601424T2** — *"Method and device for controlling crystal growth"* (with Hans Oelkrug, Wilfried von Ammon, Franz Wasmeier; filed 1996, granted 1999) — a process and apparatus for controlling crystal growth governed by measurable and non-measurable process variables.
- **EP0811707B1** — *"Verfahren und Vorrichtung zur Herstellung von Einkristallen"* ("Method and apparatus for producing single crystals") (with Hans Ölkrug, Wilfried von Ammon, Dieter Gräf; filed 1996/1997, granted 2001) — a method for pulling a single crystal in which a monocrystalline seed is brought into contact with molten material and an interface is controlled.
- A patent on modified Siemens-process polycrystalline silicon rods, in which a second-stage deposition regime producing an outer layer of larger crystallites (mean size > 20 μm), combined with sheathing the harvested rods in plastic prior to reactor removal, was shown to increase CZ crystal-growth yield from melts charged with these rods.
- A patent covering polycrystalline silicon rods with a total diameter of at least 150 mm, comprising a low-porosity core region and at least two further concentric regions of differing, specified porosity ratios — process/product claims relevant to Siemens-reactor rod quality control.
- A patent on polycrystalline silicon fragment specifications for CZ feedstock, defining tight particle-size distributions and maximum permissible dust-particle content at multiple size thresholds (< 400 μm and < 50 μm) — directly relevant to minimizing contamination and optimizing melt-down behavior in CZ pulling.

## Institutional and Field Context

Dornberger's career unfolded within a distinctively German industrial ecosystem for silicon materials science:

- **Wacker's silicon lineage**: Wacker began high-purity silicon research in Burghausen in 1953, moved to semiconductor-grade production in 1958, established the floating-zone process in 1959, and founded Wacker-Chemitronic GmbH in 1968 (renamed Wacker Siltronic GmbH in 1994, later Siltronic AG). Dornberger's career at Burghausen sits within this continuous institutional lineage.
- **Collaboration with the Fraunhofer IISB / Erlangen Crystal Growth Laboratory (CGL)**: Beginning in the mid-1990s, Wacker Siltronic's Burghausen production facilities hosted in-situ temperature-sensor measurement campaigns developed by the Erlangen CGL (led by Georg Müller and later Jochen Friedrich), including instrumented 4-inch silicon crystals fitted with thermocouples, used to calibrate the thermal-field simulation tools that underpinned Dornberger and colleagues' point-defect modeling work. Wacker was, per the historical account by Friedrich (2020, *Crystal Research and Technology*), "the only potential industrial partner on Si crystal growth" available to Erlangen CGL at the time.
- **International modeling collaborations**: Dornberger's defect-dynamics work connected Wacker's industrial dataset to academic groups including Robert A. Brown and Talid Sinno (MIT/University of Pennsylvania), François Dupret (Université catholique de Louvain), and the Russian CFD/crystal-growth simulation community (V.V. Kalaev, E.M. Smirnov, A.I. Zhmakin) — an unusually broad set of academic-industrial partnerships for a single industrial scientist's body of work.

## Legacy and Citation Impact

Dornberger's papers — particularly the 1995–2001 sequence on point-defect dynamics, the OSF-ring/$v$–$G$ relationship, and the *Defect Engineering of Czochralski Single-Crystal Silicon* review — are foundational, continuously-cited references in the crystal-growth modeling literature. They appear as core citations in:

- Nearly all subsequent point-defect parameter-regression studies (e.g., 2021 *Crystals* journal work evaluating published point-defect parameter sets for 300 mm CZ silicon).
- Reviews of numerical bulk-crystal-growth modeling spanning more than two decades, from Miller & Friedrich's 2010 *physica status solidi (b)* review to the 2024–2025 *Annual Review of Chemical and Biomolecular Engineering* article "Modeling the Growth of Bulk, Single Crystals: Seeing What Is Hidden."
- Contemporary (2013 onward) re-examinations of the Voronkov criterion for large-diameter (300 mm+) crystals, where Dornberger and von Ammon's original experimental $G$-dependence data remains a benchmark dataset.

His work exemplifies a particular mode of industrial materials science: tightly coupling proprietary, large-scale industrial process data (from Wacker Siltronic's production CZ pullers) with open academic collaboration and publication, producing results that shaped both the academic understanding of point-defect physics in silicon and the industrial practice of defect-controlled ("perfect silicon") crystal growth used throughout the semiconductor and photovoltaic industries.
