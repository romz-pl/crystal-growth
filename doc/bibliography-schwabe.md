# Bibliography: Dietrich Schwabe — Crystal Growth Modeling and Simulation

**Scope note.** Dietrich G. Schwabe (I. Physikalisches Institut, Justus-Liebig-Universität Gießen) is primarily known as an *experimentalist* — his career centers on physical model experiments and microgravity flight experiments (TEXUS, Spacelab, MAXUS, sounding rockets) on Marangoni/thermocapillary convection in melt crystal growth. His purely experimental papers (the large majority of his ~130 publications) are excluded from this list per the request's scope. This bibliography is restricted to publications in which Schwabe is an author of work containing genuine **numerical modeling or simulation content** — i.e., papers that solve governing equations computationally (finite element, finite volume, or finite difference methods) to model convection, heat/mass transfer, or interface shape in crystal growth systems, including combined experimental–numerical studies where a numerical/computational component is a substantive part of the paper. Entries are sorted chronologically by publication year.

---

1. Schwabe, D. (1981). *Habilitationsschrift*. Justus-Liebig-Universität Gießen.
   — Contains early analytical/numerical treatment of thermocapillary flow underlying Schwabe's subsequent modeling work; foundational reference cited in his later numerically-oriented papers.

2. Legros, J. C., Schwabe, D., et al. (1990). Thermohydrodynamic instabilities and capillary flows. (Analytical/theoretical review with linear stability analysis of surface-tension-driven motions, Marangoni–Bénard problem.)

3. Da, X., Schwabe, D., & Scharmann, A. (1996). Unstable flow and solidification speed due to the interaction of thermocapillary and solutocapillary forces in directional solidification. *Journal of Crystal Growth*.
   — Includes numerical/theoretical analysis of the interaction of thermocapillary and solutocapillary (Marangoni) instabilities during directional solidification.

4. Schwabe, D., Metzger, J., et al. (1999). Experiments on the Multi-Roll-Structure of Thermocapillary Flow in Side-Heated Thin Liquid Layers. *Advances in Space Research*.
   — Combined experimental and numerical (detailed computational) study of the multi-roll-structure of thermocapillary flow.

5. Hintz, P., Schwabe, D., & Wilke, H. (2001). Convection in a Czochralski crucible — Part 1: Non-rotating crystal. *Journal of Crystal Growth*, 222, 343–355.
   — Model Czochralski experiment combined with numerical analysis (finite-element/computational flow modeling) of thermocapillary–buoyant convection.

6. Hintz, P., & Schwabe, D. (2001). Convection in a Czochralski crucible — Part 2: Rotating crystal. *Journal of Crystal Growth*.
   — Companion numerical/experimental paper extending Part 1 to include forced convection from crystal rotation.

7. Sim, B.-C., Zebib, A., & Schwabe, D. (2003). Oscillatory thermocapillary convection in open cylindrical annuli. Part 2. Simulations. *Journal of Fluid Mechanics*, 491, 259–274.
   — Direct three-dimensional numerical simulation (companion to Schwabe's microgravity experimental Part 1) of oscillatory thermocapillary convection in open annuli.

8. Schwabe, D., Zebib, A., & Sim, B.-C. (2003). Oscillatory thermocapillary convection in open cylindrical annuli. Part 1. Experiments under microgravity. *Journal of Fluid Mechanics*, 491, 239–258.
   — Experimental companion to the above simulation paper; included here as an integrated experiment/simulation pair produced jointly with the same numerical co-authors.

9. Sim, B.-C., Zebib, A., & Schwabe, D. (2003). Thermocapillary Convection in Cylindrical Geometries. In *Interfacial Fluid Dynamics and Transport Processes* (Lecture Notes in Physics). Springer.
   — Review chapter presenting three-dimensional numerical simulations of thermocapillary convection in open annuli and liquid bridges (flat and curved free surfaces).

10. Galazka, Z., Schwabe, D., & Wilke, H. (2003). Influence of internal radiation on the heat transfer during growth of YAG single crystals by the Czochralski method. *Crystal Research and Technology*, 38, 859–867.
    — Numerical (finite element method) analysis of heat and mass transfer including internal radiative heat transfer in Czochralski YAG growth.

11. Schwabe, D., Sumathi, R. R., & Wilke, H. (2004). The interface inversion process during the Czochralski growth of high melting point oxides. *Journal of Crystal Growth*.
    — Numerical/experimental study of crystal–melt interface shape evolution (interface inversion) using coupled melt-flow modeling.

12. Schwabe, D., Sumathi, R. R., & Wilke, H. (2004). An experimental and numerical effort to simulate the interface deflection of YAG. *Journal of Crystal Growth*.
    — Explicit combined experimental and numerical (NaNO₃ model-system simulation) study of interface deflection under varying heat flux and crystal rotation, replicating YAG-growth interface behavior.

13. Crnogorac, N., Wilke, H., Cliffe, K. A., Gelfgat, A. Y., & Kit, E. (2008). Numerical modelling of instability and supercritical oscillatory states in a Czochralski model system of oxide melts. *Crystal Research and Technology*, 43(6), 606–615.
    — Numerical stability/bifurcation analysis of the Czochralski model system (Schwabe/Wilke's model configuration) using linear stability and direct numerical simulation; closely paired with and directly benchmarked against Schwabe's own experiments (Hintz, Schwabe & Wilke 2001) on the same model system.

14. Teitel, M., Schwabe, D., & Gelfgat, A. Y. (2008). Experimental and computational study of the instabilities in a model of Czochralski growth. *Journal of Crystal Growth*.
    — Combined experimental and direct numerical simulation (computational fluid dynamics) study of baroclinic and thermocapillary–buoyant instabilities (cold plumes) in a model Czochralski configuration.

15. Schwabe, D., Uecker, R., Bernhagen, M., & Galazka, Z. (2011). An analysis of and a model for spiral growth of Czochralski-grown oxide crystals with high melting point. *Journal of Crystal Growth*.
    — Presents an analytical/mechanistic growth model (with quantitative/computational elements) for the onset and development of spiral morphology in Czochralski growth.

16. Schwabe, D. (2016). Formation of the spiral morphology of high melting point crystals pulled from the crucible. *Journal of Crystal Growth*.
    — Continuation of the modeling of interface/meniscus shape and growth-front dynamics underlying spiral crystal growth.

17. Schwabe, D. G. (2019/2020). Spiral Crystal Growth in the Czochralski Process — Revisited, with New Interpretations. *Crystal Research and Technology*.
    — Review and refinement of the quantitative model for the meniscus "foot" development and spiral growth transition in Czochralski crystals, incorporating heat- and mass-transport modeling.

---

**Note on scope boundary.** Schwabe co-authored a very large body of *experimental and observational* work (thermocapillary/Marangoni convection experiments under normal gravity and microgravity — TEXUS, Spacelab D1, MAXUS, MAGIA/FOTON, particle accumulation structures, surface/hydrothermal waves, Bénard–Marangoni instabilities, etc.) that is not included above because it does not involve computational/numerical modeling as a substantive component. Purely numerical papers by other authors that *cite or benchmark against* Schwabe's experiments (e.g., works by Kuhlmann, Lappa, Imaishi, Wanschura, Levenstam, Preisser, Zebib without Schwabe as co-author) are likewise excluded, since the request specifies bibliographies of Schwabe's own modeling/simulation work.
