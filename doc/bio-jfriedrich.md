# Jochen Friedrich — Scientific Biography

## Overview

Dr.-Ing. Jochen Friedrich is a German materials scientist and Head of the Department of Materials at the Fraunhofer Institute for Integrated Systems and Device Technology (IISB) in Erlangen, Germany. He is one of the leading applied researchers in bulk crystal growth and epitaxy worldwide, with a career spanning more than three decades dedicated to the growth of semiconductor and functional crystals — most notably silicon, silicon carbide (SiC), gallium nitride (GaN), and compound semiconductors — combining experimental process development with numerical process modeling. He has led the Erlangen crystal growth group (successor to the historic Crystal Growth Laboratory, CGL, founded in 1974) since 2004 and has been a central, sustained figure in shaping German and international crystal growth science and its institutions.

## Education and Early Career

- Studied Materials Science (Werkstoffwissenschaften) at the Friedrich-Alexander-Universität Erlangen-Nürnberg (FAU).
- Undertook his doctoral research as a Ph.D. student of Prof. Georg Müller at the Institute of Materials Science (Werkstoffwissenschaften, chair WW6) of the University of Erlangen-Nürnberg, within what was then the Crystal Growth Laboratory (CGL), founded in 1974.
- Received his Dr.-Ing. (doctoral) degree from FAU Erlangen-Nürnberg in 1996.
- His doctoral thesis addressed crystal growth under variable gravity conditions using a centrifuge, and resolved the so-called "magic-g-level" phenomenon reported earlier in the literature — the empirical observation that crystals grown at certain intermediate gravity levels showed higher compositional uniformity than those grown at either lower or higher gravity. Friedrich demonstrated that a minimum in segregation-induced non-uniformity can occur at intermediate levels of centrifugal acceleration, clarifying a puzzle that had stood in the crystal growth community.

## Career at Fraunhofer IISB

- Joined the Fraunhofer Institute for Integrated Systems and Device Technology (IISB) in Erlangen in 1998, following his doctorate, moving from the university chair into the applied-research Crystal Growth working group that Georg Müller had established at IISB in 1996 (elevated to full department status in 1999).
- **Since 2004:** Head of the Department "Crystal Growth" at Fraunhofer IISB (renamed Department "Materials" in 2014, following Georg Müller's 2007 retirement and the full transfer of CGL's crystal growth activities into the Fraunhofer department).
- **2005:** Founded and extended the department's activities through establishment of a branch laboratory in Freiberg, Saxony — the Fraunhofer Technology Centre for Semiconductor Materials (Technologiezentrum Halbleitermaterialien, THM) — of which he became deputy spokesperson/responsible director.
- Under his leadership, the Erlangen group grew substantially in staff and scope, broadening from its historical focus (Bridgman/gradient-freeze and Czochralski growth of compound semiconductors, float-zone silicon, and microgravity crystal growth) into a comprehensive materials department covering silicon (Cz and multicrystalline/directional solidification), silicon carbide (PVT and solution growth), gallium nitride (ammonothermal and HVPE), and other functional/electronic materials, alongside continued strength in numerical process simulation (notably via the in-house CrysMAS and CrysVUn simulation software).
- His research combines a "holistic" methodology characteristic of the Erlangen school: tight coupling of crystal growth process technology, in-situ and ex-situ characterization of resulting material properties, and global/local numerical modeling of heat and mass transfer, melt convection, segregation, and defect formation, used iteratively to correlate process parameters with crystal quality.

## Major Scientific Contributions

- **Magic-g / gravity-level segregation studies (PhD work, 1990s):** Explained the non-monotonic dependence of dopant segregation uniformity on gravity level during crystal growth on a centrifuge, resolving the "magic-g" phenomenon reported by earlier researchers.
- **Microgravity crystal growth and solidification modeling:** Extensive work on global thermal and fluid-dynamic modeling of crystal growth and alloy solidification experiments performed under microgravity (parabolic flights, sounding rockets such as the TEXUS program, and orbital/ISS experiments), using and co-developing the CrysVUn simulation software; comparative studies of terrestrial versus microgravity solidification behavior.
- **Particle–interface interaction during directional solidification:** Long-running experimental and theoretical study of the engulfment and pushing of second-phase particles (e.g., SiC, Si₃N₄) at a moving solid–liquid interface during directional solidification of silicon, under both terrestrial and microgravity conditions, including sounding-rocket experiments (e.g., TEXUS 56/58) and extensions of dynamic particle-pushing models (building on the Catalina et al. framework) to compute critical velocities for the pushing/engulfment transition.
- **Electromagnetic control of melt convection:** Studies of the interaction of convective melt flows with static and rotating magnetic fields (RMF) in electrically conducting semiconductor melts, including the electromagnetic Czochralski (EMCZ) method and its effect on crystal–melt interface shape, applied to large-diameter silicon crystal growth for ULSI applications.
- **Silicon Czochralski and directional solidification process optimization:** Numerical and experimental optimization of hot-zone design and process control for silicon Cz pulling (including pushing pull speed via hot-zone optimization and analysis of growth-ridge defect formation), and technology development for multicrystalline/directionally solidified silicon ingots used in photovoltaics and as semiconductor-industry sputter targets and mechanical components; work on active crystal cooling combined with large melt volumes for cost-effective mono-Si ingot growth.
- **Dislocation formation in heavily doped Czochralski silicon:** Rigorous numerical modeling and analysis of dislocation formation during growth of heavily n-type doped, ⟨100⟩-oriented silicon crystals.
- **Quartz glass crucible behavior in Cz silicon growth:** Investigations of gas bubble evolution and cristobalite formation in fused quartz Czochralski crucibles from multiple suppliers, relevant to long-duration multi-pulling Cz processes.
- **Melt flow regimes in large-diameter Cz silicon:** 3D simulation studies of melt flow dynamics in 300 mm heavily-magnetically-doped/HMCz silicon, confirming and characterizing the "bulk circulating flow" regime and its dependence on crystal rotation rate, magnetic field strength, and melt-surface shear stress.
- **Silicon carbide crystal growth:** Research on growth of SiC bulk crystals, including recent progress on high-temperature solution growth (HTSG/TSSG) methods for SiC, alongside the department's established physical vapor transport (PVT) SiC activities.
- **Gallium nitride growth:** Work on ammonothermal GaN crystal growth (including numerical simulation of flow and heat distribution in ammonothermal autoclaves with baffle-shape optimization) and on hydride vapor phase epitaxy (HVPE) of GaN, including design-of-experiments-based modeling of species transport and growth kinetics.
- **Grain structure and defect characterization in multicrystalline silicon:** Studies correlating the grain structure of industrially grown multicrystalline silicon with the area fraction of electrically active defects, and development of characterization methods such as the "Laue-Scanner" for determination of grain orientations and grain-boundary types.
- **Materials characterization methodology:** Broader body of work linking process parameters (geometry, heater power, growth conditions, temperature and stress fields) to crystal defect formation and resulting functional properties (e.g., carrier lifetime, efficiency), forming the conceptual basis ("process–property correlation and optimization") of the department's research strategy under his leadership.

## Publication Record and Software

- Author or co-author of more than 170 publications in international peer-reviewed journals (count as of the early-to-mid 2020s; continuing to grow, with contributions appearing regularly in the *Journal of Crystal Growth* and *Crystal Research and Technology*, among others).
- Holder of more than 30 patents in the field of crystal growth technology.
- Associated with the development and application of the Fraunhofer IISB in-house crystal growth simulation codes **CrysVUn** (global/local thermal and fluid-dynamic modeling of crystal growth and solidification, including microgravity configurations) and **CrysMAS** (multi-physics simulation of industrial crystal growth processes, later extended with multi-scale dendritic microstructure models).
- Author of the widely read, open-access historical review "*Erlangen — An Important Center of Crystal Growth and Epitaxy: Major Scientific Results and Technological Solutions of the Last Four Decades*" (*Crystal Research and Technology*, 2020; based on a 2019 paper), documenting the 1974–2019 history of the Erlangen Crystal Growth Laboratory and its evolution into the Fraunhofer IISB Materials department. This paper became one of the journal's most-downloaded open-access articles.

## Institutional Context: The Erlangen Crystal Growth Tradition

Friedrich's career is closely bound to the institutional lineage of crystal growth research in Erlangen:

1. **1974:** Founding of the Crystal Growth Laboratory (CGL) at the chair of Materials of Electrical Engineering / Department of Material Science, University of Erlangen-Nürnberg.
2. **1985:** Founding of the Fraunhofer Institute for Integrated Systems and Device Technology (IISB) in Erlangen, under founding director Heiner Ryssel.
3. **1996:** Heiner Ryssel enables Georg Müller (Friedrich's doctoral supervisor) to establish a "Crystal Growth" working group at Fraunhofer IISB, bridging university and applied-institute research; Friedrich joins IISB in 1998, shortly after completing his doctorate.
4. **1999:** The working group achieves full department status within IISB.
5. **2004:** Jochen Friedrich becomes Head of the Department "Crystal Growth."
6. **2005:** Establishment of the Freiberg branch laboratory (THM) under Friedrich's co-leadership.
7. **2007:** Georg Müller retires; full transfer of CGL's remaining crystal growth activities to the Fraunhofer department is completed.
8. **2012:** Friedrich becomes Chairman of the DGKK.
9. **2014:** The department is renamed from "Crystal Growth" to "Materials," reflecting its broadened scope under Friedrich's leadership.
10. **2019/2020:** Friedrich authors the definitive historical account of this 45-year trajectory.
11. **2024:** Friedrich receives the DGKK Prize in recognition of his career contributions.

## Research Group Profile (Materials Department, Fraunhofer IISB)

Under Friedrich's direction, the department's research spans:

- Czochralski and multicrystalline/directional solidification growth of silicon for microelectronics and photovoltaics.
- Physical vapor transport and solution growth of silicon carbide (SiC) bulk crystals for power electronics.
- Ammonothermal and HVPE growth of gallium nitride (GaN) for power and optoelectronic/high-frequency device applications.
- Growth and characterization of III–V and other compound semiconductors.
- Epitaxial layer growth and device-relevant characterization.
- Numerical process modeling and simulation (heat transfer, melt/fluid convection, segregation, defect formation, magnetic field interaction) supporting all of the above, using and extending the CrysVUn and CrysMAS simulation platforms.
- Materials characterization techniques for structural and electrical defect analysis (e.g., grain structure evaluation, Laue-scanning methods).
